Minecraft: Java Edition — strategy prior for Astra, Jev and the harness

Researched 2026-09-21 for vanilla **1.21.1**, Normal Survival, empty inventory, five-minute episodes and the score specified in brief.md/interfaces.md. These recommendations optimize that task, not a dragon speedrun, PvP match or long-term building world. The timings and thresholds below are initial controller choices, not universal Minecraft rules.

**Core strategy.** Turn a short wood opening into stone equipment quickly, then secure cheap furnace/fuel/food milestones while avoiding damage. Explore only as far as necessary to find the next reachable prerequisite. Obtain exposed iron when it is cheap; stop pursuing it when terrain, darkness or time makes survival and already-known rewards more valuable. A small safe work area and reliable item pickup beat impressive but unfinished plans. The community beginner guide specifically favors a wooden pickaxe first and an early upgrade after three cobblestone; this avoids spending time making a whole wooden tool set. [Community beginner guide](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorial%3ABeginner%27s_guide), [Mojang first-day guide](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/how-survive-your-first-day).

**Opening and build order.** Treat this as a dependency graph with branches, not a rigid timed script. Code computes feasible recipes and distances; Astra chooses the next short sequence and a fallback. Jev selects among currently valid nearby targets or recognizes when to interrupt.

| Stage / rough elapsed budget | Strong action and rationale | Ownership |
|---|---|---|
| 0–15 s: orient | Inspect nearby exposed wood, accessible stone, footing, hazards and time. Select a short route to a harvestable trunk; remember a safe retreat position. Avoid wandering to an ideal scenic base. | Code computes candidates/paths; **Astra** selects a local work area; **Jev** judges which legal trunk approach is least obstructed or threatening. |
| 10–60 s: wood bootstrap | Gather about three logs initially if stone is close; convert enough to make a table, sticks and one wooden pickaxe. Place the table on accessible dry ground. Keep its coordinates. Add wood if the nearest stone requires travel. | **Code** handles quantities, recipe variants, placements and confirmation; **Astra** decides whether to work here or move toward stone. |
| 40–100 s: stone upgrade | Mine three ordinary stone blocks with the wooden pick; pick up their cobblestone; craft/equip a stone pick. Use it for subsequent stone. Avoid granite/diorite/andesite when the target is cobblestone. | **Code** checks block/drop/tool eligibility; **Astra** orders the upgrade; **Jev** may choose an exposed safe face among code-approved candidates. |
| 70–160 s: productive tools | Craft stone axe and furnace. Axe accelerates additional wood gathering and can handle nearby passive animals. Stone sword is optional if combat is likely; it has no direct milestone reward here. | **Astra** chooses axe/furnace order based on resources and threats; **code** executes the material graph. |
| 100–240 s: fuel and food | Prefer reachable surface coal; otherwise reserve logs and smelt charcoal with planks. Make torches. Start cooking local food or smelting exposed raw iron while working nearby. | **Astra** chooses coal versus charcoal and workstation placement; **code** schedules furnace jobs; **Jev** judges interruption/threat and nearby prey choices. |
| 160–280 s: optional iron | A stone pick can harvest iron. Smelt an ingot; a shield uses little iron and benefits survival. Gather up to the resource caps only if doing so is safe and cheap. Deep mining is usually a poor five-minute bet. | **Astra** weighs expected progress and remaining time; **code** estimates costs/cooldowns; **Jev** recognizes a dangerous approach. |
| Last 20–40 s | Collect finished furnace output, eat if needed, and finish cheap already-feasible milestones. Stay in a verified safe place; do not start a long cave trip. | **Code** enforces the deadline and pending work; **Astra** picks final tasks; **Jev** monitors safety. |

The bootstrap arithmetic is simple but belongs in code: three logs produce 12 planks. Four planks make the table, three make the wooden pick head, and two plank-to-stick operations consume four more planks to produce eight sticks. That leaves one plank and enough sticks for wooden pick, stone pick and stone axe. The three stone tools/workstations listed here consume 3 + 3 + 8 = **14 cobblestone** total. Gather more wood for fuel, shield, torches and reserves. Do not delay the stone pick merely to maximize the cobblestone inventory high-water score.

Different wood species have separate plank recipes even where mixed planks can be ingredients. The following is a compact planning reference. Mojang describes the table/tool opening, furnaces and shield ingredients in its [first-day guide](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/how-survive-your-first-day), [furnace guide](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/block-week-furnace), and [shield guide](https://www.minecraft.net/de-de/article/taking-inventory--shield). The [pickaxe progression](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/taking-inventory-pickaxe) explains why stone tools unlock iron. In this version, ordinary iron mining yields **raw_iron**, which must be smelted; do not search the inventory only for `iron_ore`. Avoid importing later-version copper-tool progression into 1.21.1.

One normal furnace operation takes approximately **200 ticks / 10 seconds** at 20 TPS. Coal/charcoal fuel supports eight operations; two planks support three. Code should know fuel arithmetic, remaining cook time and required inventory space, while Astra decides what to process first. Keep a reserve of sticks/planks before consuming everything as fuel. One early charcoal can supply torches or further smelting: prioritize based on available coal and the approaching night. [Smelting reference](https://minecraft.fandom.com/wiki/Smelting), [community smelting planning](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorial%3ASmelting), [Mojang charcoal guide](https://www.minecraft.net/de-de/article/charcoal).

**High-value heuristics and adaptation.** These are proposed policies grounded in the mechanics above. Numeric thresholds should be tuned on training seeds and frozen for held-out evaluation.

| Situation | Heuristic / contingency | Code | Astra prompts | Jev questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Multiple resource candidates | Prefer verified short safe paths, appropriate tool, exposed face and reliable pickup. Euclidean-nearest can be across a ravine. | Compute route/reach/drop feasibility and candidate cost. | Choose resource goal and search radius. | Choose the safest-looking approach among a few legal candidates. |
| No visible target | Explore short safe frontier segments and rescan. Mark recently failed targets; do not oscillate between them. | Spatial memory, TTL blacklist, movement/stuck detection. | Choose frontier direction based on discovered terrain and prerequisites. | Select a low-risk frontier among proposed options. |
| Tall or awkward tree | Harvest reachable low logs, then find another trunk instead of spending a minute pillaring into foliage. | Reach and support checks; bounded batch. | Permit a different tree species or nearby work area. | Recognize obstruction/hazard in candidate features. |
| Wood/stone collected but inventory unchanged | Breaking is not collecting; walk near safe dropped items, check space and wait for server inventory updates. | Pickup confirmation and timeout. | Replan only after a concrete failure. | Classify whether a pickup approach is safe. |
| No nearby coal | Make charcoal; keep a log as input and planks as fuel. Do not convert every log first. | Reserve accounting and fuel schedule. | Choose charcoal fallback versus short exploration. | No arithmetic question needed. |
| Night approaching | Work next to a small enclosed lit shelter or close a shallow alcove. Bring table/furnace nearby if affordable. | Compute time-to-sunset and return-path budget. | Decide shelter site, minimal layout and task order. | Recognize whether entry/roof/exposure is unsafe. |
| Hunger or lost health | Eat in cover; cooking increases the usefulness of many foods. Health regeneration requires sufficient food. | Exact food availability, food/health thresholds, consume confirmation. | Decide whether to hunt, cook, eat immediately or abandon risk. | Judge whether current nearby threats allow eating. |
| Furnace running | Gather nearby wood/stone, prepare shelter, or check a known resource while the input cooks. | Remember furnace position/job; compute completion estimate; keep chunks simulated. | Choose a short task that returns in time. | Interrupt if the workstation area becomes dangerous. |
| Iron nearby | Take easy exposed iron with a stone pick; don't enter a large dark cave just for a five-point component. | Harvest test and route estimate. | Compare shield/ingot progress with safe alternatives. | Rate the approach as low/medium/high risk. |
| Most progress secured | Protect health and finish pending outputs; avoid low-value novelty. | Remaining-time and marginal-score arithmetic. | Switch to consolidation. | Monitor threats and valid retreat options. |

Mojang recommends lighting, shelter, and retreat as first-night options; caves remain spawnable when dark. For this score a minimal shelter has survival value even though shelter volume is not directly rewarded. [First-night guide](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/how-survive-your-first-night-minecraft). Hunger management includes healing around food ≥18 and sprint unavailable at food ≤6; code can check these exact conditions. Treat health ≤8 as an initial retreat trigger, not a guarantee that health >8 is safe. [Hunger management](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorial%3AHunger_management), [hunger mechanics](https://minecraft.fandom.com/wiki/Hunger).

**Threats, counters and emergency priority.** Never wait for Astra to escape an imminent hazard. Jev is also too slow for some events; the code safety layer acts first when a deterministic condition is enough. Attack or defense is useful only when the relevant action has been tested on the pinned version. [Mojang mob guide](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/minecraft-mobs).

| Threat | Counter / policy | Assignment |
|---|---|---|
| Creeper nearby or priming | Increase separation along verified terrain immediately; use solid cover if available. Do not finish a log or rely on a slow melee kill. A shield is a fallback if already available and correctly faced, not a reason to stay close. | **Code** immediate cancel/escape and fuse-related metadata validation; **Jev** can recognize the highest-priority threat before an emergency; **Astra** avoids routes with repeated creeper exposure. |
| Skeleton / ranged fire | Break line of sight with solid terrain, move to cover, use a correctly faced shield if equipped. Avoid a straight exposed charge with low health. | **Code** maintains shield/route execution; **Jev** chooses among cover waypoints; **Astra** abandons a dangerous mine entrance. |
| Zombie / isolated melee mob | Prefer retreat or a protected barrier. If combat is chosen, face the target, use reach/cooldown checks and avoid being surrounded. | **Code** attack cadence and range; **Jev** recognizes outnumbering/retreat; **Astra** authorizes fighting only for a clear purpose. |
| Enderman | Avoid looking at its face and do not attack. It is neutral until provoked; do not turn every detected entity into a combat target. | **Code** gaze/target restrictions; **Jev** recognizes neutral versus hostile context; **Astra** routes around. [Mojang Enderman](https://www.minecraft.net/en-us/article/meet-enderman). |
| Lava, fire, deep fall, gravel/sand overhead | Avoid entry and unsafe digging. Never mine the supporting block directly below the player or dig blindly upward under falling material. | **Code** geometry and block rules every tick; **Jev** flags ambiguous exposure; **Astra** chooses another face/path. |
| Water / drowning | Preserve a reachable shore/air route; stop underwater mining and surface before air is low. Do not treat an unknown water landing as automatically safe. | **Code** immediate air/position handling; **Jev** recognizes a bad crossing; **Astra** goes around when feasible. |
| Hunger/health deterioration | Stop sprinting when food is scarce; eat in safety; avoid attacks while food/health recovery is needed. Raw chicken/rotten flesh carry food-effect risks; prefer ordinary cooked foods when available. | **Code** food effects and thresholds; **Jev** safe-to-eat judgment; **Astra** food acquisition plan. |
| Several simultaneous threats | Escape lethal terrain/drowning first, then imminent attacks, then stabilize hunger/health, then resume work. Never retreat blindly off a ledge. | **Code** vetoes unsafe candidate actions; **Jev** bounded tactical choice; **Astra** strategic reset of the task. |

Java combat has weapon-dependent attack cooldowns; repeated clicks are not full-strength repeated attacks. Keep cooldown timing in code, derived from the current item's attack-speed data, and have a conservative fallback if data is unavailable. Jev should not count elapsed milliseconds. Neutral/passive target classification belongs in a registry plus context, not a blanket `nearestEntity()` attack. [Community combat guide](https://minecraft.wiki/w/Tutorial%3ACombat), [Mineflayer attack interface](https://github.com/PrismarineJS/mineflayer/blob/4.39.0/lib/plugins/entities.js).

**Astra prompt responsibilities.** Give Astra a compact verified snapshot, the current goal/phase, exact ingredient deficits, legal action candidates with costs, score components/marginal gains, remaining time, threats, workstation jobs, map memory and precise recent errors. Do not ask it to reconstruct quantities from an unstructured event log. Ask for a small bounded plan: goal, 2–5 subgoals, resource reserves, allowed Jev options, stop conditions, fallback, and plan expiry. The harness validates the response against its action schema.

A useful planning instruction is: “You have the stated remaining wall time. Select the next achievable early-game milestone and a safe fallback using only known resources and legal actions. Preserve fuel/tool/food reserves supplied by the controller. Give conditions under which this plan must stop. Unknown terrain is not safe terrain.” Replan after a milestone, unreachable target, changed resource availability, serious threat, or expired plan. Do not issue another slow planning call for every individual block.

The skill-library lesson from Voyager is to compose reusable behaviors with execution feedback and explicit postconditions. For this harness, keep the runtime actions fixed and validated; Astra may design/improve their implementation in the development phase, but during an episode it should select typed actions rather than inject arbitrary JavaScript. [Voyager architecture](https://github.com/MineDojo/Voyager). Our two-model scheduling and safety design is a benchmark proposal, not a reported Voyager result.

**Jev typed-question contract.** Each batch uses one snapshot/episode/plan ID, a freshness timestamp and only currently legal candidates. Code supplies calculated distances, reachability, hazard flags, cooldown readiness, hunger status, score gain and route estimates. Jev recognizes situations and makes bounded choices; it does not invent coordinates, plan a crafting chain, perform arithmetic, or bypass code vetoes.

| Typed question | Allowed result | What code provides / does next |
|---|---|---|
| “Does the current work area show a threat that warrants interrupting this job?” | boolean | Nearby threats with distance/closing flags, cover and job state. Code already interrupts deterministic emergencies. |
| “Which listed threat deserves attention first?” | enum of threat IDs plus `none` | Registry classifications, visible/occluded status and already-computed imminent-threat flags. |
| “Which approved escape waypoint is safest in this scene?” | enum of 2–4 candidate IDs plus `none` | Only collision/path-validated candidates; selecting none invokes the deterministic fallback. |
| “Which of these reachable tree approaches is least exposed?” | enum of candidate IDs plus `none` | These are alternative targets for Astra's existing wood goal, not alternative strategic goals. |
| “Is this candidate work position suitable to continue crafting/eating?” | enum `safe`, `unsafe`, `uncertain` | Threat visibility, cover, footing and exit features. Uncertain prevents a new vulnerable action. |
| “How risky is this proposed short gathering route?” | enum `low`, `medium`, `high` | Geometric feasibility already checked; Jev recognizes contextual exposure. |
| “Does the observed job state suggest obstruction rather than useful progress?” | boolean | Code-derived stuck/no-progress flags plus recent environment changes; exact timeout remains code. |
| “Which nearby passive animal approach is least risky?” | enum of candidate IDs plus `none` | Valid passive prey only, reachable terrain and current food goal from Astra. |

Batch independent questions in one call. Do not ask Jev to predict the outcome of another answer in that same batch. Revalidate the chosen ID against the newest state, discard replies from old episodes/plans, and expire tactical advice quickly (initial target about one second). No legal candidate means no model question is necessary. A missing answer leaves the bounded job or safe fallback running; it does not stall physics.

**Common mistakes and their concrete corrections.** Each item specifies the principal owner.

- **Code:** Equating `canDigBlock` with “will drop the wanted resource.” Check `canHarvest` and the expected drop before mining; a wrong-tier tool can destroy an ore without useful yield. [Digging implementation](https://github.com/PrismarineJS/mineflayer/blob/4.39.0/lib/plugins/digging.js), [block harvesting API](https://github.com/PrismarineJS/prismarine-block).
- **Code:** Confusing recipe operations with result count. Four planks are one recipe execution, not four. Serialize crafting windows and verify output before continuing.
- **Code:** Navigating into the solid log/table block, or relying only on distance. Find a reachable stand point with a visible interaction face; re-read the target after travel.
- **Code:** Treating a resolved dig or a vanished item entity as confirmed inventory gain. Inspect actual server updates, capacity and pickup results.
- **Code:** Using pathfinder defaults that allow destructive travel, parkour or tall drops. Start with conservative movements and explicit gathering permissions; reapply settings if plugins replace them. [Movement source](https://github.com/PrismarineJS/mineflayer-pathfinder/blob/2.4.5/lib/movements.js).
- **Code:** Timing out a Promise while its action continues. Use real cancellation, ownership tokens and neutral controls; no old job may resume after replacement.
- **Astra:** Overplanning an iron/diamond progression while easy table, tools, torch and cooked-food milestones remain. Optimize reachable marginal progress within the remaining seconds.
- **Astra:** Burning every log/plank or leaving the only table behind. Reserve upcoming ingredients and decide whether moving a workstation is worth the collection/travel time.
- **Astra:** Treating village looting, beds or sheep as mandatory. They are opportunities only when close; travel can exceed the value within five minutes.
- **Astra:** Starting a large house, crop farm, mine shaft, animal breeding or Nether trip. Their payoff is usually beyond this episode; use a minimal shelter and nearby resources.
- **Jev:** Selecting an apparently nearest target despite code-provided hazard/context flags. Ask about exposure among legal candidates, then let code veto stale/unsafe selections.
- **Code + Astra:** Returning to a furnace too late or wandering beyond its simulated area. Keep a return budget and only schedule nearby work while cooking.
- **Code:** Farming the score through item transfers, duplicate tool crafting or placed-block loops. Use resource high-water marks and one-time item milestones, with no reward for attempts or duplicate possession.

**Evaluation priorities for harness search.** Tune the controller on training seeds for successful wood→stone conversion, productive use of furnace waiting time, reliable cancellation, recovery from no-path failures and survival during dusk. Measure milestone times and progress points separately from survival credit. Ablate optional combat, wide exploration, aggressive movement and Jev calls: if a feature adds failures without consistent progress, simplify it. A meaningful held-out test uses unseen world seeds and repeats for dynamic randomness; repeatedly optimizing one cached landscape is memorization, not robust Minecraft play.
