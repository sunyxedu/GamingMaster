Hypothesis: the leader's remaining rare-achievement gap is a post-upgrade scheduling failure. In the 0002 trace for seed 1101, the step-281 observation records `iron_pickaxe: 1, diamond: 0`, while diamond is not collected until step 512; in seeds 1104 and 1106 an iron pickaxe was crafted but no diamond appeared before the episode ended. Seed 1102 is the sharper failure: its final step 577 observation records `iron: 1, iron_pickaxe: 0, diamond: 0` after the player dies. The normal planner can spend this window on an iron sword, stale adviser objectives, or a route cycle. A short code-owned diamond expedition immediately after the observed iron-pickaxe gain should increase diamond collection without changing the early dependency chain.

What changed versus 0002: the harness records inventory transitions. When `iron_pickaxe` increases while diamond is still absent, it arms a bounded 110-tick/32-action expedition. During that window, the existing verified pathfinder targets known diamond and otherwise exposes new map frontier through `acquire('diamond')`; each action is re-observed, and combat, food, drink and sleep safety still take precedence. The lock clears immediately on diamond collection or on expiry, after which the ordinary planner and asynchronous Astra advice resume. Malformed observations, model replies and action errors remain contained by the parent's parser and loop.

Evidence that would confirm or refute it: traces should show `make_iron_pickaxe` followed by a concentrated diamond search, fewer episodes holding an iron pickaxe with `collect_diamond` still zero, and no increase in death during the bounded window. The hypothesis is refuted if the lock mostly walks already explored terrain, prevents iron-sword or survival achievements, or leaves diamond rates unchanged. The discriminating comparison is the 20–110 ticks immediately after the first iron-pickaxe inventory increase: diamond discovery or genuinely new map cells should replace stale objective/cycle actions.
