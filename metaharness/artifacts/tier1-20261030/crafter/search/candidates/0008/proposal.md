Hypothesis: the leader’s remaining misses are conversion failures caused by long rare-resource expeditions. In seed 1102, the leader unlocked 15 achievements by step 179, placed a furnace at step 422, and only collected iron at step 536; the episode then ended at 577 without an iron pickaxe or sword. In seed 1108 it reached 15 achievements by step 186, then ended at step 227 with no coal or iron. The controller can find the dependency chain, but its known station is often too far away when a rare resource is discovered, so hunger, mobs, or map boundaries terminate the run before crafting.

What changed versus 0002: rare-resource acquisition now uses a checkpointed expedition mode. Once iron or diamond becomes the active missing resource, the harness records a nearby legal stand position and prefers to place a temporary table/furnace within the current mapped radius before walking farther. It caps each rare-resource leg at a reserve-aware distance, returns to the checkpoint immediately after the first inventory gain, and verifies the gain before allowing the next tier. The model remains advisory and cannot extend a leg beyond the code budget. Existing map/facing bookkeeping, recipe-delta verification, hostile handling, and malformed-advice containment are retained.

Evidence that would confirm the hypothesis: training traces should show an iron/diamond gain followed within a short bounded leg by a successful iron craft, fewer episodes ending with iron in inventory but no iron tools, and no increase in starvation deaths. It would be refuted if temporary stations consume time/resources without shortening returns, if rare-resource achievements fall because the distance cap prevents discovery, or if failures still end with a rare resource held but no legal station. The discriminating trace is a `rare_checkpoint` action sequence: station placement, resource gain, return, then craft success.
