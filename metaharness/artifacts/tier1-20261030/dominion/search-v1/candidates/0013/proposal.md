Hypothesis: the leader optimizes final VP margin in its rollouts but overrides that objective with win-only ending gates in live execution. Refusing every losing finish can enlarge a loss; taking every winning finish can forgo profitable scoring. A complete-turn endgame decision that compares exact ending margins with sampled continuation margins should remove this mismatch without changing the successful engine construction.

Evidence: in candidates/0008/trace_seed1201.jsonl line 113, decision 98 on turn 17 has $11, five Buys, one Province, and public VP 17–36. Province plus Estate guarantees 24–36 (margin -12), but the leader buys Duchy, then another Duchy, because its losing_end guard excludes Province. At line 125, decision 110 on turn 19, $9 and three Buys can end 30–40 (-10), but it again buys Duchy. The actual game ends 30–50 (-20). These observations establish available, better-than-realized finishes; they do not establish that continuing was worse in expectation. Candidate 0010's race guard did not fix the broader problem: its mean is 12.833 versus the parent's 13.333, and its worst loss is -21.

Change versus parent: retain the complete acquisition-policy portfolio, public-belief forward model, action/thinning executor, bounded asynchronous Astra advice, and strict legal-ID handling. Replace win-only ending overrides near termination with complete purchase-basket search. Enumerate affordable scoring and pile-ending combinations, include pass and economic continuation candidates nominated by the existing policies, calculate every ending's VP margin exactly, and compare nonending baskets by complete-game sampled margin. Use a separate validation sample batch for finalist continuations and retain an exact finish unless the sampled improvement exceeds one sampled standard error (with a 0.5 VP minimum). Commit only the remainder of the selected current-turn basket, rebuilding option IDs at each prompt. No new Province race threshold, payload heuristic, seed-specific rule, or private state is added.
