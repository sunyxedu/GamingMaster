Hypothesis: a complete-policy portfolio can conflate taking a scoring opportunity with abandoning engine construction. Its build-to-$16 policies have no opponent-ownership input, so they keep investing when the opponent already controls the Province race. Separating opportunistic Province harvesting from the rest of engine construction should reduce the leader's catastrophic race loss without forcing premature Duchies or disabling profitable engines.

Concrete evidence: in 0008/trace_seed1201.jsonl, line 44 (decision 34, turn 11) buys Laboratory with $8, five Provinces remaining, and public VP 0–21. The opponent already owns three Provinces. Line 52 buys Market with $8 on turn 12; line 61 buys another Market with $9 on turn 13; line 74 buys Market with $11 on turn 14. These are full-turn missed Provinces, not purchases preceding another Province in the same turn. The portfolio forecasts compare laboratory_build's investment to other policies' Province purchases, coupling current scoring with different future acquisitions. The agent first scores on turn 15 and finishes 30–50. The trace supports a timing failure; it does not prove that any one missed Province would win.

Change versus parent: preserve 0008's public-belief model, complete-policy search, action/trash/discard executor, legal-ID validation, exact winning ending basket, and nonblocking bounded Astra advice. Revise the two build policies so they buy an affordable Province when the opponent owns at least three Provinces and leads our Province ownership by at least two. At smaller budgets, or after closing the Province deficit, the original engine construction remains active. Apply this public-state condition both at the live root and every simulated buy. Do not force Duchy/Estate conversion, change thinning, use turn/kingdom/seed identifiers, or increase the portfolio size. Existing immediate-scoring money/engine policies remain alternatives. This is a targeted structural separation of engine construction from race response, not a global scoring threshold adjustment.

Confirmation: completed error-free games, fewer affordable Province passes under the disclosed lead condition, and improved final margin in races while preserving strong engine margins when the opponent is not ahead. Refutation: harvesting Provinces cripples draw before the engine can recover, the portfolio responds by avoiding build policies entirely, or race margins regress despite better immediate VP. Public Province count is a conservative urgency signal, not a proof of optimal play; hidden draws remain uncertain. A successful result would motivate comparing several interleaving policies, while a failure would point toward acquisition/deck-cycle modeling rather than broader hard scoring gates.
