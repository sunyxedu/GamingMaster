Change: retain 0004's ordinary legal action/trash/gain policy as the continuation policy, but replace its stationary endgame evaluator with public-zone Monte Carlo purchase search from the opening onward. Each hypothetical world subtracts known hand, discard, and played multisets from ownership, verifies the remainder against deck_count, and samples only the unknown order. Cleanup, draws, discard reshuffles, new gains, attacks, thinning, supply depletion, and full-turn endings are propagated. Opponent hidden zones use a clearly labeled exchangeable approximation and the disclosed BMU-1 action/buy policy. No game implementation or hidden state is accessed. Each candidate first purchase is evaluated with common estimator seeds through a complete-game continuation; paired uncertainty shrinks apparent improvements toward the local baseline. Purchases and extra buys are reconsidered from every new observation.

Astra receives concrete rollout alternatives, public rules, recent acquisitions and forecast residuals at opening, the first Province-race transition, and on failures. It can disable an unreliable rollout model, change the uncertainty penalty and scoring thresholds, or repair the current prompt with validated IDs. Calls have a 300-second model timeout and a separately bounded 320-second future wait, above the observed 216-second maximum. At most four reviews preserve time to finish; a fresh failure can launch one recovery request behind a stuck call. Replies from obsolete decisions cannot supply direct actions. Malformed output and model exceptions preserve legal local execution.

After every step the harness checks ctx.progress(), advancing decision IDs, rejection counts, and purchase effects. It compares predicted next-turn coin distributions against observed buys, verifies VP arithmetic and own-zone conservation, and asks Astra to replan after repeated failures or economic collapse. All simulated orders are hypothetical and no estimator random seed is a game/reset seed. Forecasts are approximations: opponent zone assignment ignores some public-event information and future purchases use a fixed local continuation rather than recursively optimal play.

Confirming evidence: improved terminal margins, especially when early purchases or late support cards would miss the relevant shuffle; completed legal games; traces showing predicted next-turn spending tracks actual spending and purchases differ for states with different zone allocations. Refutation: repeated forecast mismatch, overconfident investment in a policy that cannot realize rollout gains, poorer margins than 0004, or CPU cost jeopardizing completion. Remaining approximation limits: the opponent's random zone allocation does not condition on its previous public plays or purchases; future agent decisions use the local continuation policy rather than recursively applying purchase search; current-turn remaining buys before an ending use that same continuation. Simulated games are capped at 36 additional turns per player, and any cap hit is logged rather than represented as a true game finish. These can bias estimates even when Monte Carlo error is small. The sampling penalty accounts for sampling error, not model error; Astra can switch off search when observed evidence contradicts it.

A planning-interface lesson from the final traces: Astra twice compared projected terminal margin directly with the current VP margin and called the gap optimism.
