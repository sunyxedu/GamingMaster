Hypothesis: the remaining bottleneck is the authority boundary between planning and execution. Broad regimes and small priority bonuses can silently defeat Astra's intended acquisitions. The leader's worst games (0001 seeds 1400/1401) tie at 30–30 and 31–31, but all eight opening calls log `strategy_wait_cutoff` at 200 seconds: its +5 mean measures fallback behavior, not successful strategic guidance. A concrete counterexample to the regime interface is 0002/1300: Astra returns `{"mode":"money","priority":["Silver","Gold","Province"],"targets":{"Silver":2,"Gold":3,"Province":4}}`, yet decisions 1 and 2 both buy Chapel. At turn 14 it owns `{"Chapel":6,"Copper":2}`. Astra asks again for treasure, but the final deck includes ten Chapels and the margin is -75. Legality and changing observations did not identify strategic failure.

This is a new branch from the direct Astra controller 0000, not a refinement of the leader's numeric ranks. Astra now writes a short executable acquisition agenda, valid for four own turns: ordered `[card, total_owned_cap]` entries. The first affordable eligible entry is the purchase; there is no hidden blending with numeric support scores. Duplicate entries can stage purchases. Gains can have their own agenda. Code shows counterfactual purchases at $2/$3/$4/$5/$6/$8, audits actual ownership after execution, and logs the agenda revision that caused every acquisition. Explicit Province/greening guards are part of the model's documented contract. Routine sequencing, card choices, cost/VP arithmetic and current prompt IDs remain local.

Replanning occurs at agenda expiry, a Province-race transition, economy collapse, three successive sub-$3 turns, an exhausted agenda despite spending power, unfamiliar prompts, rejected/unchanged steps, or an acquisition effect that disagrees with public ownership. Every step checks `ctx.progress()`. Eight reviews maximum, at most six ordinary expiry reviews, leave time for completion and recovery. Model-authored agendas expire after four own turns; if a replacement is unavailable, the executor rebuilds a conservative fallback from the current public deck instead of repeating expired targets indefinitely.

Calls have a 240-second backend timeout, above the recent 117-second worst and historical 130 seconds. An independent future wait allows up to 900 seconds for the opening and 300 seconds subsequently, always reserving at least 240 seconds of the remaining episode for local completion. This separate queue allowance is evidence-driven: 0002/1300's first request took 363 seconds despite 81 seconds of reported model latency; 0005/1200's first took 1,325 seconds despite only 51 reported. Therefore backend timeout/latency alone cannot be the completion watchdog. At most one unresolved routine request is retained; a concrete execution failure permits one additional recovery request. Outstanding futures are tracked, stale agendas/IDs cannot overwrite current choices, and model failures preserve legal local play.

The tactical supplement is exact current-buy-phase ending enumeration, without hidden-order simulation or future-value forecasts. It evaluates Gardens thresholds, remaining buys and supply counts, continues buying after a pile becomes empty, and only overrides with a non-losing terminal bundle. Public VP verifies the arithmetic; a discrepancy disables this search and is sent to Astra. Otherwise the published agenda controls purchases. Thinning preserves a model-specified floor of total treasure money, and mandatory gains do not silently become arbitrary alphabetical cards.

Confirmation: complete games with actual accepted/executed agendas, no repeated purchases past model caps, useful revisions after economic/race surprises, and improved worst-seed margins relative to the direct-call parent and regime failures. Refutation: well-executed agendas still fall behind the opponent, four-turn commitments miss crucial changes, or Astra plans consume so much time that games stop before completion. If only fallback runs, that tests fallback strength but does not confirm the authority-boundary hypothesis.
