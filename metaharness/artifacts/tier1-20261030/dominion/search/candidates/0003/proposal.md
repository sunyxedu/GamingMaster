Hypothesis: the weak score is primarily a control-bandwidth failure. The baseline asks Astra for one action per call, and the traces show 20–27 calls consuming the entire 1800 second episode; the worst episodes end in the middle of an ordinary action phase while still behind. Astra often gives valid short answers, so spending another call for every predictable continuation is the expensive part. A short, decision-id tagged plan should let the harness execute several safe steps per call and reach the Province race before the deadline.

What changed vs. the parent: Astra is prompted to emit 3–8 `choose` commands. The harness validates every command against the current prompt (decision ID, legal option IDs, and min/max), executes one at a time, and invalidates the remaining suffix whenever a nested effect, attack, public change, illegal action, or stalled observation appears. It includes compact previous-state and `ctx.progress()` evidence in replanning prompts, catches model failures and illegal actions, and uses a legal first-option/pass fallback so arbitrary preflight output cannot crash the episode. The model still owns strategic choices; code owns validation and recovery.

Evidence that would confirm the hypothesis: similar or better VP margins with substantially fewer System-2 calls, more completed turns, and fewer deadline endings, especially on the 1201/1401-style weak seeds. Evidence against it: plans frequently become stale after ordinary turns, or batching causes enough poor acquisitions/illegal responses that VP margin falls despite finishing more turns; then the next design should shorten plans or specialize prompts around nested effects.
