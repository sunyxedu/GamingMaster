## Hypothesis
The leader's primary failure is control-loop throughput: expensive single-action calls end episodes before the deck is explored. In seed 12, the terminal trace has score 9, deck_size 24 and all three lives at the 1800-second deadline; seed 14 has score 9, deck_size 23 and three lives. Seed 12 steps 3 and 4 are rejected `REVEAL_COLOR 1` and `REVEAL_COLOR` actions at the unchanged turn 2. This is wasted reasoning and wall time rather than unavoidable game loss. The baseline also routes both seats through one model session, which violates the required private-information boundary.

A reliable executable communication policy plus exact bookkeeping should finish games and score substantially higher while needing only occasional Astra judgment. The principal experiment is to move routine decisions and protocol execution out of the model loop, retaining Astra as a bounded strategic adviser.

## Changes
- Exact multiplicity-weighted joint own-hand marginals via subset dynamic programming, including negative clue information, visible-card subtraction, and public discards/stacks. Critical cards and unreachable color ceilings are computed in code. These marginals are exact under the finite-copy prior conditioned on literal masks and public promises; they do not model the selection bias of every earlier policy action. Partner assessments use public-only counts, not an invented private view.
- A deliberately simple shared convention: rank clues are literal/save clues; color clues promise a deterministic focus is immediately playable. Focus is the oldest newly color-clued card whose public domain permits the next rank, otherwise the oldest eligible touched card. Sender rejects clues that would make a false promise. Other touched cards receive no play promise. Promise identity is derived from the public color clue and pre-action stack, never copied from the sender's private view.
- Automatic certain plays, low-loss discards, clue scoring by newly enabled safe plays and threatened unique cards, and final-round risk checks. Each action is re-evaluated against the new observation; no stale slot plans.
- Up to three short, validated option-selection calls, at least five turns apart; calls bypassed on certain plays, less than 900 seconds remaining, or clear choices. A 90-second backend timeout, a 120-second outer wait on a ctx.spawn future, and an autonomous completion reserve address latency. After any model exception/outer timeout, all further decisions are scripted; outstanding model work is cancelled by the evaluator at episode close. Timeouts and malformed answers use the scripted choice.
- Separate model sessions for absolute seats; only public protocol deductions persist in common memory. No seed, deck order, engine internals, or other-seat private history is read.
- Actions are exact dictionaries from legal_actions; rejected actions have a finite retry limit.

## Confirming / refuting evidence
Confirmation: normal deck-exhaustion endings, materially higher scores than 10.25, fewer than 40 calls, no illegal actions, and no false protocol plays. Refutation: losses of reachable low-rank last copies or clue congestion dominate despite completion, or the limited color-focus channel cannot deliver needed plays. A future iteration should distinguish insufficient inference/communication from poor discard valuation using decision logs; do not respond by merely increasing model frequency.

## Validation
Pre-flight passed: complete game, 22 points, 64 actions, zero model calls (this is a correctness check, not a score estimate). A focused check script verified 100 randomized exact-marginal cases against exhaustive enumeration, protocol truthfulness on archived observations, malformed/out-of-range replies, and timeout/runtime fallback. Two dev attempts were started, then interrupted by the proposer while model requests remained blocked; neither supplies a completed dev score. Both attempts predate the final outer-wait fix. The first trace revealed a 510-second request-to-return gap for a call reporting 24.8 seconds of backend latency; this motivated reducing the adviser cap from ten to three and reserving half the wall budget for completion. A fourth request in the original ten-call dev version also waited several minutes. The final revision therefore additionally bounds total request wait using a managed model future. No training or held-out evaluation is run by the proposer.

The final outer-wait branch was exercised with a deliberately stalled fake future: it returned the fallback, marked the adviser budget exhausted, and made no further request. Final pre-flight again completed at 22 points in 64 legal actions. It made zero adviser calls, so model integration is supported by the focused checks rather than claimed to have been exercised by pre-flight. The initial dev trace reached turn 24, score 5, deck 26, and three lives without an illegal action; it was interrupted during its fifth request. These partial figures are not a final performance estimate.
