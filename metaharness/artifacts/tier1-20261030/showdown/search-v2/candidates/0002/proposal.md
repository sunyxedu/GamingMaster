Hypothesis: the baseline fails because it does not turn strategic reasoning into an executable, state-dependent matchup policy. A fresh one-action answer every turn is expensive, yet it also repeats avoidable tactical mistakes. Compiling named, guarded rules for a matchup should finish substantially more battles and react more reliably to changing HP, boosts and forced replacements than either single-action calls or a positional action batch.

Evidence: all ten baseline training episodes ended at the 1800-second deadline; the worst, seed 333667, executed just 14 decisions and scored -0.093552. Its public trace shows Volcanion using Steam Eruption, Haze, Steam Eruption against Giratina and dropping from 256 to 51 HP before switching to a healthy Clefable, whose Dragon immunity then blocked Dragon Pulse. Later Clefable repeated resisted Moonblast into Typhlosion and took 198/293 damage from Eruption. Raichu's repeated Thunderbolt, by contrast, safely finished the weakened Typhlosion. These are conditional matchup decisions, not a universally safe fixed sequence.

Change: fresh design, using the baseline only as diagnostic evidence. Astra compiles ordered rules covering named own Pokemon against the current revealed foe. Guards include HP, opposing HP, stat stages, status, forced replacement and usage limits. Moves and switches are resolved by name against the latest request, never saved slot indices. Policies are reused across turns and switches. A local approximate type/damage/speed evaluator provides fresh attack rankings and a legal fallback; hidden stats are explicitly estimates, not engine knowledge. Strategic status/setup, preservation, switching and Tera remain Astra's decisions. Compact fresh-session prompts avoid replaying duplicate full histories.

Each action is verified using public events, HP changes and ctx.progress(). Rejections, heavy damage, immunity/failure, dangerous boosts, opponent Tera, no-progress repetition and switch cycles revoke the policy and provide evidence to Astra for replanning. Calls are limited and a wall-time reserve is kept for code execution; 210-second model timeouts exceed the archive's observed 124-second maximum. Malformed text and call failures fall back to current legal choices.

Confirm: more completed battles, substantially more decisions per model call, fewer repeated losing exchanges, and wins on diverse matchups without harness errors. Refute: brittle rules frequently invalidate, approximate fallback repeatedly misjudges hidden coverage or mechanics, or extra throughput merely completes losses. Policies and estimates must be assessed on public trajectories and unseen teams; no seed-specific behavior or hidden data is used.

Dev evidence (initial version): `dev/20261031-102225` completed seed 869431 normally in 1696 seconds: 33 decisions, seven System-2 calls (six answered, one caught 210-second timeout), score -1.041667, no harness errors. Five opponents fainted and one remained. This supports battle completion and policy reuse, but does NOT demonstrate a score improvement. There is no paired baseline dev result and one battle is insufficient to estimate win rate.

The dev trace falsified the initial failure detector's use of net HP loss. On decision 3, Giratina took 190/349 from Night Slash on entry, which was omitted because the active identity changed. On decision 5, Strange Steam dealt 152/349, but Leftovers reduced the net loss below the alarm threshold. Rest then concealed another 158/349 incoming hit, and the policy left Giratina asleep in a losing matchup. A separate critical Gengar exchange showed that the two-action replanning cooldown could execute a fallback action before asking Astra, even after revoking a dangerous policy.

Final refinement: parse direct incoming move damage separately from recovery and residual effects, including newly switched-in targets. Revoke a policy on a large direct hit or a likely lethal repeat unless a fast estimated KO is available; critical contradictions bypass the ordinary replanning cooldown. Sleeping Rest rules are skipped. Prompts now emphasize special attacks versus burn, Rest's two sleep turns, and compare every living teammate's top approximate attacks. Two observation-driven arithmetic fixes treat an omitted Showdown level as 100 and discard stale boost stages on benched teammates.

Final validation: pre-flight passed and finished normally (35 actions). It reports no answered System-2 call because the short dry run falls below the model-call wall reserve; parser coverage therefore comes from separate stand-in checks for garbage text, malformed JSON, model TimeoutError, and valid policies over archived trajectories. All 137 baseline decision states produced legal fallback actions. Named switch remapping, disabled-choice filtering, public Tera, level omission and bench-stage checks passed. Targeted dev replay recovered all three incoming damage amounts above exactly and raised critical replanning evidence, including damage masked by healing. The final refinement has not received a second live dev evaluation; its playing-strength effect remains unmeasured.
