Hypothesis: the leader loses mainly because its one-action-per-Astra-call control loop is too slow to reach an endgame. The traces show 7–17 accepted choices in 1800 seconds, with roughly 45–60 seconds spent before nearly every action; all training episodes therefore ended at the deadline while still ongoing. This prevents the model from converting even a favorable opening and makes the final score mostly an HP snapshot after a handful of turns.

What changed vs. the parent: Astra now receives a compact but decision-focused ledger (private request, legal actions, public revealed state, and recent events) and returns a bounded line of up to three exact commands. The harness commits those commands one at a time, validating each against the newly returned legal_actions list because switches reorder slots and pivots/forced replacements create extra requests. Any rejection, unchanged observation, stalled score/observation progress, request mismatch, or other progress evidence clears the line and triggers replanning. Arbitrary model text is parsed by matching only legal command names, with a first legal action fallback on call failure, so pre-flight garbage cannot escape as an error.

Evidence that would confirm or refute it: compared with the parent, accepted decisions should rise substantially (and more episodes should reach a terminal result before the deadline), while System-2 calls per episode fall by about the line length. If scores remain near zero despite completed battles, or traces show repeated bad continuations after valid observations, then latency was not the dominant failure and the next iteration should improve state interpretation or tactical search rather than batching.
