# Hypothesis: useful temporal abstraction needs an expected-observation contract

Candidate 0003's matrix is an expensive way to select one move: its training mean was -1.111 with eight timed-out calls. Its repetition guard requires an already-revealed move, unchanged context, substantial outgoing damage, nonzero incoming damage, and no revealed resisting bench Pokemon. Thus it often pays for a new decision even when the last answer already explains the correct continuation. Candidate 0001 improves outcomes, but still spends many calls on stable tactical exchanges and then delegates late-game play to a much weaker fallback. Counting its training trace events gives 247 Astra actions, only 14 checked continuations, 85 fallbacks and 9 sole-legal choices (355 actions total). Parent 0003 has 202 matrix actions, 101 fallbacks and 12 sole-legal choices, with zero verified repeats.

Concrete leader evidence: in `0001/trace_seed232003.jsonl`, turns 12, 13 and 14 all choose Scald versus Rotom-Fan. Turn 13 explicitly says Lanturn survives boosted Air Slash; that hit takes HP from 1.000 to .730 while Scald takes Rotom from .52 to .14. Turn 14 repeats Scald and survives the observed reply. In the same trace, replacement Scyther is explicitly selected to finish 9% Mamoswine with Bug Bite; its checked follow-up actually executes, showing that some decisions can be compiled safely. In `0001/trace_seed333667.jsonl`, turns 35-37 repeatedly choose Dazzling Gleam against Grimmsnarl, yet the closing battle uses heuristic fallbacks. Conversely seed 232003's opening forecast names hazards/Lava Plume, but actual Solar Beam KOs Swampert. Plans must distinguish predicted new information from unpredicted threats, not simply ignore reveals.

This is a different experiment from refining the leader's damage hints or the parent's matrix weights: replace one-shot counterfactual scoring with a bounded model-authored feedback policy, then measure whether reasoning survives later into the battle.

# Changes from parent

- Keep the parent's public observation helpers, ordinary type chart, contextual damage/move-order ledger and legal fallback. Remove payoff matrices and weighted selection entirely. Astra chooses a single action and is asked to compile a narrow conditional follow-up for an ordinary attack or switch whenever a safe region exists. A null policy must identify a concrete tactical obstacle. No generated code is executed.
- A policy names an ordinary attack, optional immediate recovery and threshold, expected enemy move IDs, minimum own HP, an optional maximum enemy HP, a conservative total HP-cost bound, and one to three extra decisions. It binds identities and tactical state, then remaps move IDs to current legal slots. It can cover replacement/switch then attack, repeated attack, or heal/attack feedback.
- Explicitly anticipated newly revealed moves do not invalidate a policy. Unexpected moves, changed identities, status, boosts, types, items, unexpected weather/terrain/hazards, unusual mechanics, rejected/failed actions, critical hits, exhausted PP or violated HP bounds do. Gross observed HP cost, including recoil/residual before healing, tests the forecast. A chosen entrant's known Surge terrain is accepted only when its protocol activation and resulting field match; suppressed terrain or any additional field change cancels the policy. A fresh Astra call receives the failed assumption and evidence.
- Every action checks `ctx.progress()`. Two ineffective attacks or stalled observations invalidate a local policy; all policies expire after at most three extra choices. No policy autonomously switches, spends Tera, repeats setup or conditional/charging moves, or spans an opponent change.
- State reports preserve the entire recent protocol before structured current state, own team, move descriptions and memory. Public item/ability evidence supplements summary omissions. Exact private HP/items override public caches, and a factual Tera-resource field overrides stale strategic memory. Model calls have 240-second timeouts, above the current archive's 178-second worst latency. Timeout and malformed responses recover through legal fallback and subsequent replanning; one call and an engine-time reserve remain available.

# Confirmation and refutation

Support requires materially more verified policy actions, fewer calls per meaningful exchange, and later Astra coverage of new threats without increased tactical losses. Logging identifies compiled policies, their actual executions, and exact invalidation reasons. Improved training mean and weak-seed outcomes would be stronger evidence than dev wins. The policy contract must reject the Solar Beam-style surprise and remap changing team slots correctly.

Refute if policies almost never execute, if output/guard complexity consumes the saved latency, or if expected-move assumptions let the opponent exploit stale decisions. The local policy is not a hidden-set solver; an initially bad action still loses before any guard can save it. A short budget reserve may still force late fallback. No seed-dependent decisions, external data, engine imports or hidden state are used.

# Validation and dev result

Final preflight passed with no warnings: one answered stand-in System-2 call, 21 legal actions and natural game completion. `check_harness.py` and `checks.json` record 26 checks, including full-loop recovery from injected TimeoutError/RuntimeError and malformed replies, plus report/fallback legality on 198 complete archived actionable observations (159 truncated snapshots skipped).

A real archived Piloswine switch accepts the explicitly anticipated first Icicle Crash reveal and chooses Earthquake; a move-slot permutation remaps it to move 3. Coverage surprises, gross damage/recoil concealed by healing, new passive items, Tera/status/boost changes, immunity, misses, unavailable moves, expiry and `ctx.progress()` failure signals cancel the policy. Controlled terrain fixtures accept the known entrant's protocol-confirmed activation while rejecting an added Trick Room, suppressed activation, or unconfirmed change. These are mechanism checks, not playing-strength measurements.

One real dev evaluation ran: `dev/20261031-154000/`, seed 869431, **loss, -1.062977**, 30 turns / 35 decisions, 28 Astra calls, no model failures or episode errors, 1574.65 seconds. Median successful call latency was 50.93 seconds; maximum 131.29 seconds. The opponent retained four Pokemon and .251909 team HP fraction. The completed digest and traces were inspected before the final prompt/terrain revisions. No second dev run or full training evaluation was started.

The dev build (SHA `248210c60e9186a6180e086b35f6e3f22f787a613e56c38d2ce992d811c7f5ab`) returned null policies on 27 of 28 model calls. Concrete diagnostic evidence:

- The lone compiled policy was invalidated by `Weather, terrain, screens or hazards changed.` The observation shows `|-fieldstart|move: Grassy Terrain|[from] ability: Grassy Surge|[of] p1a: Rillaboom`. That is a predictable consequence of the chosen entrant, not an unexpected opponent response.

Final revisions make conditional continuation the primary requested artifact, shorten optional explanation/memory, default to one extra decision, require a concrete reason for null, and log whether a policy was offered and compiled. They also handle verified own-entry Surge terrain, preserve passive-item/illegal-action evidence, count recoil/residual HP cost, and expose Tera usage explicitly. The dev process predated these final changes; **the final revision is locally checked and preflighted but has not been evaluated with real models**. The principal unresolved question is whether more explicit elicitation produces useful, safe policies rather than simply more invalidated plans or longer calls. The observed loss is retained as negative evidence, not relabeled as success.
