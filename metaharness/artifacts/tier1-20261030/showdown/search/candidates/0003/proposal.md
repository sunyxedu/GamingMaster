# Hypothesis: optimism about a single line loses to simultaneous adversarial choices

The baseline's weak seeds contain avoidable tactical commitments, not just latency problems. In `trace_seed333667.jsonl`, steps 1–3 use Steam Eruption, Flamethrower, then Haze: Giratina first uses Calm Mind, then Dragon Pulse takes Volcanion from 256 to 135 to 17 HP. Giratina returns to 99% while a healthy Unaware Clefable was available. At step 11, Clefable selects Fire Blast against Tinkaton, but Gigaton Hammer KOs it before it acts. That removes the proven Dragon immunity. In `trace_seed308081.jsonl`, steps 4–5 repeat Calm Mind after Vileplume switches into Suicune; Giga Drain then KOs Suicune (a critical hit, so not proof of a guaranteed ordinary KO). Step 15 uses Nasty Plot into Beartic's already revealed Earthquake and Spectrier falls from 240 to 3 HP. These are concrete examples of evaluating our move without sufficiently pricing the opponent's strongest response and the next turn.

This is a fresh controller, not a parameter refinement of the leader. The experimental intervention is an **adversarial response matrix**: each Astra call evaluates three distinct legal actions against two named plausible opponent responses, including attacks, switches and tactical alternatives. The controller chooses `0.65 * expected value + 0.35 * worst plausible response value`. This exposes optimistic setup and attacks that never execute, while retaining some upside when behind. Scores include the best next reply, essential counters and Tera, rather than raw damage. Response probabilities and evaluations are judgments, not simulator truth.

## Harness changes

- Fresh self-contained calls replace a growing conversation containing repeated full logs. The prompt preserves the exact private request, current switch slots, all public sets, current effects, recent protocol, move descriptions and a short strategic memo.
- An empirical ledger compiles direct damage, critical/faint censoring, move order, recent actions, and failed actions from the public stream. Direct damage is distinguished from poison, entry hazards and recoil. Samples retain their boost/item/ability context and are presented as conditional observations, not exact future damage.
- A transparent ordinary type chart supplies arithmetic; it does not claim to model all abilities or damage. Model evaluation handles tactical mechanics and hidden-set uncertainty.
- One legal action executes without a model. A model-authorized attack may repeat once only after substantial observed damage, observed incoming damage with ample survival margin, unchanged matchup/effects, no disruption, and no obvious revealed resisting bench pivot. Every action is checked via both protocol and `ctx.progress()`, with failures supplied to Astra on the next available call. Opponent moves/switches absent from the named response labels are flagged as evidence of a missed threat (a conservative textual check, not semantic proof). No setup, recovery, Tera or unchecked action sequences are batched.
- The 180-second timeout exceeds the archive's 104-second maximum. After a timeout or schema failure, subsequent calls use a shorter two-action matrix with no per-option explanation. Timeouts, malformed matrices and illegal/rejected choices recover. Incomplete matrices are rejected rather than interpreted as ranked lists. If all available damaging moves are immune and switching is legal, fallback switches rather than repeating zero damage. Bench move/type multipliers are supplied even on forced replacements, to expose immunity errors before selecting a teammate. Calls reserve more than 185 seconds of wall time; the final time window uses a legal type/HP fallback rather than starting a doomed call. One model call is left unspent to avoid exhausting the call budget. The fallback is explicitly weaker than the matrix and continues checking effects.

## Confirmation / refutation

Support would be fewer losses of essential counters, fewer setup turns immediately followed by avoidable KO, and better weak-seed outcome/HP margins against the same opponent. Matrix logs should show a plausible opponent threat reducing the chosen value of an otherwise tempting attack/setup. Repeated-action traces must show observed safety and immediate return to planning after new evidence.

Refute the hypothesis if matrix values remain optimistic despite the evidence, if downside weighting repeatedly yields passive switching/recovery cycles, or if larger model responses consume the wall budget without improving completed battles. The response model is not a mechanics simulator; its quality is the principal experimental risk. Dev seeds are diagnostic only and never become branches or stored matchup prescriptions.

## Validation and dev evidence

Final pre-flight **passed without warnings**, answered one System-2 stand-in call, and ended naturally. The first pre-flight had found a terminal `request: null` dereference; it was repaired. Checks passed for malformed/NaN/incomplete matrices, robust-choice selection, revealed opponent Tera typing, type-chart immunities, legal fallback membership and 193 readable archived transitions. Archive truncation prevented checking 108 serialized observations. Synthetic repeat checks verify cancellation on move-slot identity change, newly revealed move, status, unsafe HP, critical hit and changed species.

One real dev evaluation ran on seed 869431 (`dev/20261031-133238/`): **score -1.104135**, natural loss after 33 decisions / 1753.6 seconds; 12 System-2 calls, four caught 180-second timeouts, no episode error. Successful calls had median 82.7 seconds and maximum 102.1 seconds. This is not positive outcome evidence for the hypothesis. The dev build preceded the final Tera-field, repeat-guard, shorter-output and immunity-fallback fixes; the final revision has not received a second real dev evaluation.

What the trace established:

- The opening matrix omitted actual Body Slam, then incorporated the observed move and damage. Its next choice, Knock Off, covered a switch to Galarian Weezing and removed Leftovers. Its third choice preserved Rillaboom by rejecting recoil into a likely lethal response.
- Larger matrix calls cost substantial time; four timed out. Fallback handled these without an error, but controlled most of the closing battle. The final prompt asks for two response columns, three actions, <=220 visible tokens, qualitative values and at most eight words per rationale; after a timeout it drops to two actions and <=150 tokens. This is a latency mitigation to test, not a measured improvement.
- One response had three scenarios but two values per row and falsely claimed Shadow Ball could revenge Normal-type Wyrdeer. The final parser rejects this malformed matrix; bench ordinary type multipliers now expose the immunity even on replacement requests.
- Late fallback repeatedly attacked into immunity. The final fallback escapes if every damaging move is immune and a switch is legal. Replay of the dev observations found seven such states now choosing a legal switch. This is a general mechanics fix, not a seed-specific policy.

Only one dev evaluation was used. No training/held-out results or implementation source were accessed. The final pre-flight and archive/mechanism checks pass, but improved playing strength remains unestablished.
