Hypothesis: Astra's raw-observation policy loses primarily because it chooses a promising move without checking whether the user survives long enough to execute it, or whether sacrificing that Pokémon removes the only answer to an opposing win condition. A compact counterfactual combat card plus observed-damage feedback will prevent these errors better than repeating larger histories or simply increasing planning length.

Evidence from the archive: in candidate 0000, seed 333667 steps 1–3, Steam Eruption and Flamethrower only chip Calm Mind Giratina while Volcanion drops 256 → 135 → 17 HP. Step 4 then demonstrates the available healthy Clefable's Dragon immunity. At step 11 Clefable selects Fire Blast but Tinkaton's Gigaton Hammer KOs it before it moves. The end state has all six foes alive. In seed 282617, steps 5–6, Lurantis takes Brave Bird to 42/264 HP and repeats Leaf Storm; the next Brave Bird KOs it before it attacks. These are decision-quality failures even in episodes that finish well before the deadline, not just latency failures.

Fresh design, not a refinement of the leader's recipe:

* Each stateless Astra consultation receives compact authoritative state, current move descriptions, a short strategic memory, recent protocol, eight decisions and observed direct-damage/move-order evidence. It does not accumulate a growing session of duplicate full observations.
* An item ledger supplements the cached summaries with explicit passive item reveals and removal events. The current private request remains authoritative for own held items; a removed item is not confused with an unknown one.
* A standard-library combat calculator supplies estimated damage for each teammate's attacks, speed references, entry hazard damage, hypothetical 90-BP opposing STAB probes and Tera changes. Public types/levels/stats/moves come only from the observation. Hidden stats use an explicitly labeled neutral reference; probes are hypotheses, not inferred moves or asserted bounds. Variable-power and unsupported mechanics are left to Astra. This is tactical decision support, not a replacement simulator.
* Astra must audit two-turn survival, opponent response and preservation of the key check before choosing a legal action. Its concise strategic memory carries counter assignments and uncertainty between decisions.
* Astra may authorize up to two guarded repetitions of an ordinary attack. Execution checks current legality, both identities, effects, newly revealed information, HP thresholds and actual foe HP reduction. Every action is followed by ctx.progress() and protocol inspection; rejection, stale observations, lack of progress, a large HP loss or changed mechanics invalidates the plan and sends the evidence to Astra.
* A bounded tactical fallback handles malformed answers or transient failures, and a 180-second model timeout exceeds the archive's 104-second worst latency. One call is reserved so a long battle can continue with legal fallbacks instead of ending at the hard 40-call limit. No inference outside ctx, no hidden-state files or seed-specific logic.
* The consultation admission check measures elapsed episode-clock time around calls, including time outside reported backend latency, and reserves 1.2 times the largest observed call plus 90 seconds for finishing play (at least 180 seconds total). The first dev call used about 177 seconds of episode time versus 55.8 seconds of recorded model latency, motivating this general timing safeguard.

Confirmation: fewer attacks that fail because the user faints first; earlier preservation/use of unique checks and defensive Tera; damage surprises lead to changed plans; improved worst-seed outcome/HP margin. Observe whether compact stateless calls and verified repeats reduce wall-clock/call exhaustion without increasing tactical errors.

Refutation: the approximate combat cards mislead Astra about unmodeled abilities, coverage, items or speed; the same sacrificial errors persist despite explicit audits; or repeats accelerate losing exchanges. Score improvements caused only by more unfinished ties would not establish better battle play.

Validation so far: preflight passed (21 actions, normal game completion; its short wall budget exercised fallback only). Replayed 204 archived actionable observations through combat-card rendering, prompt construction and fallback legality, and checked malformed/JSON/plain-action parsing; largest prompt was 16,365 characters. A separate mocked eight-action model-path run recovered from injected TimeoutError, RuntimeError, garbage, malformed action values and invalid repeat values without escaping exceptions. Type immunities/dual-type multipliers and passive-item reveal/removal behavior were checked. One real dev evaluation is running; results will be appended below.
