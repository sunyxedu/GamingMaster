Hypothesis: the leader's sampled, three-gamble continuation policy introduces avoidable estimation error in small endgames. When the complete posterior is small, an exact observation-conditioned decision tree can optimize all remaining safe progress, remove Monte Carlo ranking noise, and recognize genuinely equivalent moves. This does not make an irreducible 50/50 solvable.

Trace evidence: 0008 loses with just one safe cell left on seeds 602, 608, and 611, scoring 380/381, 380/381, and 84/85 respectively. Its final `guess_lookahead` entries give both remaining alternatives risk 0.5 and expected gain 0.5. Earlier in seed 611, the four candidates in a four-cell endgame have estimated expected gains 0.7299, 0.7132, 0.7689, and 0.7520, despite identical root risks. The finite sampler is distinguishing small differences with standard errors around 0.022; exact enumeration can tell whether those differences are real. These are posterior-uncertainty losses, not evidence of arithmetic errors or proof that a different tied choice is better.

Change versus parent: preserve its opening, live constraint deductions, and large-position rollout policy. Before sampling a small endgame, enumerate every complete mine layout consistent with visible clues and the total mine count. Partition each candidate reveal by its complete observable result, including zero floods. Recursively optimize expected additional safe reveals until terminal, with memoized posterior/hidden-cell states. Hidden layouts only generate feedback; subsequent actions depend on the observation partition. Evaluate all available actions, without the parent's six-percentage-point risk band or three-gamble horizon. A bounded search that cannot finish falls back to the unchanged parent policy; partial worlds or partial trees are never called exact.

Confirmation: small endgames produce reproducible exact values, equivalent actions receive identical values, and synthetic exhaustive checks agree with an independent decision-tree oracle. Higher progress on new endgames with useful information probes would support the strategic hypothesis. Refutation: all encountered endgames are genuinely symmetric, the search seldom finishes, or apparent score improvement comes only from changing the endpoint of a 50/50. Any training gain from exact ties must be reported as tie luck, not stronger inference. Pre-flight and permitted dev evaluation results will be recorded below.

Implementation: attempt complete posterior enumeration only with at most 18 unproved hidden cells and 4096 compatible worlds. Cartesian products of component models and unconstrained-cell combinations enumerate each full layout exactly once, conditioned on the global mine count. The search has a 12-second maximum, reduced by remaining episode time, and a 120,000-state cache limit. Cancellation is checked while enumerating, building feedback, and descending the tree. All search state is episode-local.

The decision-tree value is an integer sum of safe-cell gains over equally weighted worlds. The branch observation includes every newly revealed coordinate and its clue; a zero flood is one observation, not a guessed single clue. Exact integer comparisons avoid roundoff and Monte Carlo noise. Certain-safe moves are processed first: observing a guaranteed-safe cell cannot reduce attainable expected progress. A canonical safe choice avoids factorial searches over equivalent safe orders. Among equal expected-progress actions, use expected action count under this safe-first policy, then the parent's minimum-risk/geometric ordering. Expected progress is exact; the secondary action-count criterion is not a proof of globally minimal clicks because safe-order permutations are pruned. No model calls, seed access, speculative flags, or new background work are introduced.

Initial validation: required pre-flight passed with score 1, 38 actions, and zero model calls. `verify_synthetic.py` matched every root action's exact gain against an independent, unpruned observation-tree oracle on 86 distinct synthetic endgames. It also checked complete posterior membership against brute-force combinations, supplied known mines, enumeration-order invariance, rejection of incomplete posterior enumeration, and cancellation. These tests import no game implementation.

Archive diagnosis, using only the playing observations before the recorded guesses: seed 611's four-cell posterior has four equally likely layouts. All four root actions have exact expected gain 0.75, confirming that the parent's 0.7132–0.7689 spread is sampling noise. Seed 602's six-cell posterior also has four layouts: its four related upper-edge choices have exact gain 1.25 each, while either cell of the separate pair has gain 1.0. The parent already picked the better group. All three final two-cell posteriors contain two layouts and value either action at 0.5. Thus these specific failures do not establish room for higher expected progress; they establish that the new method recognizes equivalence and avoids spending hundreds of simulations on tiny posterior sets. Any changed outcome on these ties is luck, not a solved ambiguity. No training episode was rerun.

Dev evaluation: one permitted run, `dev/20261030-191610/`, won both episodes without errors or model calls: 2403 in 34 actions / 7.71 seconds, 2404 in 114 actions / 13.39 seconds. The digest and full guess diagnostics were inspected. Both episodes used the inherited rollout once, then finished entirely by deduction; neither exercised the new endgame branch. Their identical choices, values, and action counts versus 0008 verify preservation of the large-position policy, not the endgame hypothesis. The independent synthetic oracle and the archived playing-observation analysis provide the direct checks of the new branch. No second dev run or full training evaluation was performed.

Limitations: exact planning applies only below conservative posterior/cell/time limits. Larger positions still inherit finite-horizon sampling, candidate screening, and approximate probabilities when component enumeration is incomplete. Even exact Bayesian action selection cannot infer a hidden endpoint in an observationally symmetric 50/50. The proposal makes a stronger small-state decision procedure available for unseen endgames; it does not claim that the near-perfect training leaderboard has a statistically meaningful residual improvement available.
