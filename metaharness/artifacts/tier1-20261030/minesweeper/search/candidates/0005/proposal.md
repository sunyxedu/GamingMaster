Hypothesis: minimizing immediate mine probability, followed by a geometric tie-break, leaves useful progress on the table. Guesses should also account for the size of a possible zero flood and the safe cells that the next clue would prove. This is a predictive-planning experiment, distinct from the other proposals' opening and boundary preferences.

Evidence from the archive: 0001's seed 601 starts at `reveal 8 8` with `revealed:3`, then makes nine one-cell reveals before losing on `reveal 8 12` with `safe_revealed: 9` out of 216. Candidate 0006's corner opening improves its mean, but `trace_seed607.jsonl` records `(0,0)`, `(15,15)`, `(0,15)` and terminal `safe_revealed: 2` out of 216. Its first corner shows `revealed:1`: adjacent cells have mine probability 1/3, while the remote pool has probability 39/252. Moving away is therefore defensible. The untested assumption is that the lowest-degree remote square also yields the most progress. A corner can be zero more often while revealing a smaller region than an edge midpoint. These losses motivate the hypothesis; they do not prove the selected guesses were statistically wrong.

What changed vs. the parent: the corner opening, exact residual/subset deductions, global mine-count coupling, and model-free control remain. When no safe deduction exists, a new posterior predictive planner samples 384 complete layouts satisfying every visible clue and the global mine total. Component assignments are weighted by the number of completions in other components and the unconstrained pool. Sampling uses a fixed local RNG independent of the environment seed; no layout or outcome from the archive is used in play.

The planner compares up to roughly 19 frontier and geometric candidates within six percentage points of the lowest exact mine risk. For each candidate it estimates the safe cells opened by its flood fill, plus the safe cells proved by applying the constraint solver to the hypothetical single new clue. It ranks `(1 - mine_risk) * estimated_gain / mine_risk`, with a modest sampling-uncertainty discount. This is a short-horizon progress-per-hazard heuristic, not a claim of optimal play. Mine risk comes from exhaustive counts; sampled outcomes never become safety proofs. A genuinely exact zero-risk reveal bypasses planning.

Each search has node and time caps, checks cancellation, and discards incomplete enumeration. If all frontier components cannot be enumerated, the planner falls back to the parent's risk-based choice; the inherited fallback can approximate oversized components. The control loop commits one action and rebuilds from the returned observation. It does not call Astra, parse model text, create speculative flags, or cache across episodes. Repeated unchanged observations stop the loop rather than spinning to the deadline. Guess logs record candidate probabilities, estimated gains, chosen/baseline actions, sample count, and search time.

Validation: required preflight passed with score 1.0 in 38 actions and zero model calls. Two permitted dev runs were used. An initial fixed frontier-premium sketch won 2403 but lost 2404 at 1/195; it was replaced with the predictive planner rather than tuned to those coordinates. The final planner won both dev seeds (34 and 114 actions), with no errors and no model calls. In the final 2404 trace, it compares `(0,10)` and the baseline `(11,19)` at identical exact risk 0.1822034, estimates conditional gains 12.942 versus 10.889, and chooses `(0,10)`. That reveal opens 32 additional safe cells. The final dev run is `dev/20261030-181616/`; two seeds are insufficient to establish generalization.

Independent synthetic checks compared exact probabilities and deductions against brute force on 80 small constraint systems: maximum marginal error was zero and every inferred fact held in every compatible layout. A 4096-draw disconnected-component check had maximum marginal-frequency error 0.01489, and confirmed-mine exclusion, size-cap fallback, and cancellation checks passed. These tests use synthetic constraints only and do not run the game.

Confirmation would be larger score jumps following guesses, improved low-progress episodes, and decision logs showing useful gains among equally safe choices without excessive search cost. Refutation would be lower training progress despite accurate probability accounting, persistent overestimation of sampled floods, or frequent fallback on the very states where planning matters. Full training evaluation is left to the evaluator.
