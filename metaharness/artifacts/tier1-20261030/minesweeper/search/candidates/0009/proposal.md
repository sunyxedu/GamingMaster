Hypothesis: a single-clue, immediate-gain planner misses the value of the chain of observations that follows a reveal. Candidate 0005 divides immediate expected gain by mine probability, but the evaluator rewards total safe progress before a loss. A posterior rollout that actually reveals the hypothetical zero region, exhausts deductions, and continues through several guesses should better value moves that start a solvable frontier.

Evidence: leader 0006's `trace_seed607.jsonl` reveals `(0,0)` and `(15,15)`, both `revealed:1`, then loses at `(0,15)` with `safe_revealed: 2` of 216. Parent 0005's `trace_seed610.jsonl` similarly reveals `(0,0)` and `(11,10)`, both `revealed:1`, then loses at `(11,0)` with 2 of 195 safe cells. Its guess log estimates conditional immediate gain 12.192 at the chosen cell, versus 12.191 at another equal-risk candidate. These observations motivate testing continuation value; they do not establish that either losing guess was statistically wrong.

Change: preserve the parent's corner opening, constraint deductions, posterior completion sampler, and exact root mine probabilities. Replace its one-observation gain-per-hazard ranking with bounded posterior policy rollouts, using only hypothetical visible observations for simulated decisions. Sampled layouts are used to simulate action outcomes, never to prove live cells safe. Every real action is followed by replanning. No seed or archived mine layout enters the policy.

The rollout estimates additional safe cells obtained after the candidate and up to three subsequent guesses, exhausting bounded residual/subset deductions between guesses. It simulates the whole visible zero region and every safe reveal's new clue. The continuation policy uses inexpensive local constraint marginals and a residual-pool estimate to select guesses. Those approximate rates rank hypothetical guesses only; they cannot assert safety or become live knowledge. This avoids using the sampled world as an oracle, but makes policy quality a limitation of the value estimate. The hypothesis concerns continuation value, not optimal Bayesian play.

There are up to 160 posterior worlds per decision. Each world is evaluated for every candidate before its results enter the estimate; an interrupted round is discarded for all candidates. Candidates need at least 12 surviving root samples each. The ranking is `(1 - exact_root_mine_probability) * (mean_additional_safe_cells - 0.5 * standard_error)`, with a nonnegative floor. Root death contributes no new safe cells, and downstream losses retain their accumulated progress, matching the scoring rule. The parent instead divided immediate gain by mine probability. We retain its six-percentage-point root risk band and coverage of corners, edge midpoints, center, and nearby frontier, with six frontier candidates to bound the more expensive search.

Every search is limited to 35 seconds or the remaining episode budget minus four seconds, whichever is less. Enumeration retains the parent's size, node, and time caps. If posterior enumeration is incomplete, or there are too few balanced samples, choose the deterministic risk baseline. No incomplete counts become proofs. The live loop also rebuilds residual equations after adding proven mines, so global weighting cannot use stale residuals. Guess logs include exact risk, conditional gain and standard error, expected gain, continuation losses, completed worlds, chosen/baseline cells, and elapsed time. There are no model calls, asynchronous tasks, speculative flags, or cross-episode state. The unchanged-observation guard stops failed-action loops.
