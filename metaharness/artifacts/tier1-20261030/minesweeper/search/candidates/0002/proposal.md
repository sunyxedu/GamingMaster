Hypothesis: the leader's low mean (0.350) is caused primarily by paying for a slow Astra decision on every turn and then trusting guesses that are not checked against the visible frontier.  The trace for seed 600 shows one initial request followed by the 1800-second deadline with zero actions.  In seed 611, Astra had reached 76/85 safe cells but selected `reveal 2 4`; the terminal board shows that square was a mine, so the episode stopped at 0.894 despite enough local information to avoid an arbitrary guess.  Repeated speculative flags also make the baseline's state harder for the next prompt to interpret.

This candidate replaces the per-turn model loop with an exact, code-side solver.  It opens the guaranteed-safe center immediately, builds eight-neighbour residual equations from every revealed number, applies zero/full residual and subset-difference inference to a fixed point, and flags only cells proven to be mines.  It chords numbers whose proven flags satisfy the count.  When no forced safe cell exists, it enumerates each small connected frontier component and ranks cells by conditional mine frequency, using a global remaining-mine rate for unconstrained cells.  It catches action and budget exceptions and does not depend on model output, so arbitrary pre-flight replies cannot break parsing and model latency cannot consume the episode before the first action.

Evidence that would confirm the hypothesis is a large reduction in zero-action deadline episodes, fewer losses after a high progress score, and higher minimum as well as mean score while using zero System-2 calls.  It would be refuted if exact deductions still lose early on training boards, or if the component-size cutoff causes frequent uninformed guesses and a model-assisted strategy scores better on the same seeds.
