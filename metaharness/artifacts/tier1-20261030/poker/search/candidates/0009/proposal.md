## Hypothesis and evidence

This tests a different architecture from the leader's all-street particle posterior: a specialized river endgame attached to 0006's fast uniform-equity baseline. ## Implementation

Enumerate the 1,081 possible hole-card combinations excluding only the public river board. Rank each exactly with the parent's standard-library evaluator. For each hypothetical opponent hand, compute its exact equity against its own uniform legal opposing holes by subtracting the rank distributions of hands sharing either of its cards. There are 990 legal opposing hands in that calculation. Hero cards remain unknown to that hypothetical opponent, as required by its information boundary. Then remove hero-blocked opponent combinations, leaving 990 actual possibilities for hero's range calculation.

Weights use coarse preflop priors and the observed river action sequence. Checks, value bets, mixed bets, calls and raises receive likelihoods based on the documented .62/.85 free-bet and .80 raise gates. Sizing buckets, smoothing and likelihood floors preserve uncertainty about clipping, policy details and earlier selection. The river rank and uniform equity calculations are exact; the posterior weights are explicitly approximate and do not condition on flop/turn actions.

Compare fold, call and legal candidate bet sizes in terminal chip EV. A potential opponent raise is an aggregate branch with a single optimal fold/call continuation, never a separate clairvoyant decision for each hidden hand. Large bets beyond 1.5 pots are eligible only with estimated called equity >= .97; an additional small cost regularizer discourages near-tied larger bets. Checking uses showdown value as an approximation; an out-of-position check may face a later wager and is re-solved on the next observation. This limitation may overvalue checking.

The parent still owns preflop and non-river play, with stronger flop/turn calling floors against value-sized wagers and higher equity requirements for postflop raises. Astra retains bounded posture reviews and receives failures plus public evidence. Calls now time out after 180 seconds, above the observed 121-second maximum, with a 240-second reserve. Parsing and inference failures are caught; every action is legalized and followed by `ctx.progress()` checks. Failed execution requests a fresh Astra review, and all future actions continue to undergo the same checks. No game implementation or hidden information is accessed.
