Hypothesis: the current leader has already solved the uniform-prior expected-reward planning problem, but its scalar value function hides avoidable lower-tail risk among equally good policies. Distributional Bellman planning can protect weak outcomes without the expected-reward sacrifice made by candidates 0002/0003. This tests cost-free downside protection, not a claim to beat the Bayes-optimal mean.

Evidence: in the leader's seed 11 trace, `[1 2 3 4]` and `[1 3 2 5]` both receive `0 black peg(s), 3 white peg(s)`. Twenty public-rule-consistent secrets remain. Its `[2 4 5 3]` final guess has predicted mean 0.5375 but realizes 0.5; all verification events report `ok: true`. These are uncertainty failures, not errors that more prompting can repair. The robust alternatives both score 0.5625 overall versus the leader's 0.578125 and sacrifice uniform-code mean (about 0.639/0.637 versus 0.664931). A risk-first objective has a measurable cost.

Changes: retain the leader's public-information belief tracking, exact TicTacToe minimax, canonical symmetry opening, action validation, one-action execution, and Astra recovery. Replace scalar Mastermind planning with a nine-bin terminal reward histogram in integer eighths. First maximize its exact total reward. Among ties, lexicographically minimize the count of the lowest reward, then the next-lowest, and so on. Histograms add across feedback branches, allowing an exact two-step Bellman search and preventing sacrifice of mean. Final decisions still search all legal guesses, including eliminated secrets. A marginal-count pass identifies all mean-optimal final actions; only those need distribution evaluation. No random relabeling, training-code lookup, prior fitted to seeds, or arbitrary risk parameter is used.

The second move compiles a contingent policy with each feedback branch's exact belief, action, and predicted histogram. The third move checks that its public feedback matches a certified branch and rechecks the action and histogram before execution. Every actual move still checks ctx.progress(), rejected actions, observation change, native move count, action consumption, and board/clue consistency. Any discrepancy switches to Astra with the full public evidence; malformed replies and model errors are caught and followed by a legal fallback. Repaired actions are verified too.

Confirmation: exhaustive simulation across all 360 abstract public-rule secrets should preserve the leader's exact total reward and reduce counts in its lowest occupied reward bins.
