Hypothesis: a model can retain strategic control of NetHack if it purchases *bounded transactions of useful game actions* rather than individual keypresses. The baseline is primarily blind and bandwidth-limited: in `trace_seed1101.jsonl`, the opening request ends inside `glyphs` with `…(truncated)`; four opening answers are `wait`, and its final state is still (22,18), time 3, score 0 after eight commands. In `trace_seed1106.jsonl`, the final message is `In what direction?`, time 5, after eleven commands. An expensive call per ungrounded keypress cannot explore even the first floor.

This is a fresh model-led macro planner, rather than the autonomous exploration / sparse-adviser designs being proposed in slots 1 and 2. Astra explicitly orders bounded goals (navigate, open door, explore, search, fight, heal, eat, descend) and authorizes which observed creature species may be fought in that transaction. Code compiles each goal into single verified actions. Geometry and prompt completion are deterministic; strategic goal selection returns to Astra when the transaction finishes, encounters an unapproved adjacent creature, loses substantial HP, changes floors, or ceases making progress. A fixed exploration policy operates only inside an explicitly requested bounded macro, or as a degraded mode after model failure/budget exhaustion.

The model sees named stats and messages first, a complete numbered ASCII map, exact inventory letter-to-action mappings, observed creatures with glyph/color/pet flags, reachable goal candidates with distances, search coverage, and the previous transaction's outcomes. Independent compact calls carry only a short note and recent outcomes instead of a growing chat. Replies are data-only JSON and cannot introduce unsupported actions or executable code. Plans are capped at 160 commands between model decisions, and every command is re-observed.

The movement graph learns blocked edges from commands that neither move nor advance game time. Pet highlighting and open-door glyph/color correspondences are grounded in archived observations and remain subject to movement/message feedback. Door directions and edible inventory letters use only exposed command keys. More is sent only for observed text screens; unknown corpses and peaceful-attack confirmations are declined. Timeouts and other model failures fall back to a bounded deterministic plan; the last model call is reserved to avoid ending execution simply by exhausting the call budget.

Confirm: multiple useful actions per call, nonzero score on weak seeds, and successful completion of model-selected goals with few invalidations. Compare against slots 1/2: if similar action throughput but better survival/score emerges, model ownership of goals matters. Refute: most transactions terminate after only one or two actions, model latencies still dominate, or low-information contact interruptions consume the budget. Logs distinguish plan selection, checkpoints, model failures and unresolvable prompts. This design may underperform fully autonomous control when a map requires many cheap local decisions.

Validation: initial complete harness written before dev evaluation. Pre-flight and observed dev evidence will be appended below.

Initial validation: pre-flight passed (`game_done`, 1,062 actions, no errors). Its short virtual budget admitted no model call, so separate mocked-context checks covered garbage replies and TimeoutError recovery (20 valid commands after each), malformed JSON field types, and routing through the archived open doorway in seed 1102. Review also fixed duplicate belief updates on plan-completion loops and prevented degraded-mode loops when no reachable goal remains. A two-seed dev evaluation was started at 09:19 UTC; results pending.

Dev-informed refinements (the running dev processes retain the initial version): seed 3304's first plan executed 39 commands, collected 4 gold and reached a new creature at full HP before reconsulting Astra. Its first answer arrived at harness clock 599 seconds although the model-call record reports only 41.6 seconds of inference. Seed 3305's first answer arrived around 959 seconds with 11.4 seconds reported inference. The final harness therefore starts with one guarded 24-command exploration transaction, so a queued opening call cannot prevent all early movement and visible-gold collection. Goal ownership returns to Astra after this small setup exception.

The seed 3305 trace also exposed a concrete graph failure: `You stop.  Your kitten is in the way!` was recorded as a permanent failed movement edge when the pet had moved out of the attempted destination in the returned observation. The resulting prompt showed no reachable goals despite a known corridor back to the room. Final code treats pet-block messages as transient, clears obsolete blocked edges when terrain is revealed, and treats the observed backtick boulder symbol as an obstacle (the initial run had pushed one into a dead end). Targeted checks verify pet blockage versus solid-stone blockage, passage revelation, boulder avoidance, and the damage guard after healing. These are general observation-grounded corrections, with no seed/coordinate-specific policy.

Completed dev evaluation: `dev/20261030-091916/` reports mean score **11.5**, no fatal condition and no episode errors. Seed 3304 scored 23 after 129 commands and 3 model calls (2 completed plans); seed 3305 scored 0 after 44 commands and 6 calls (5 completed plans). Both reached the wall deadline at depth 1 with full HP. Seed 3304 gained experience and gold; seed 3305 repeatedly selected reasonable eastward goals that the erroneous graph rejected. The result supports the throughput mechanism but does not establish that model-owned goals outperform autonomous strategy, nor that the final corrections improve held-out play.
