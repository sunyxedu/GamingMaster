Hypothesis: the zero-score plateau is an observation and control-bandwidth failure. The baseline serializes arrays before game state and truncates the result. All eight initial model_request prompts are 8,450 characters long, end inside the glyph array with `…(truncated)`, and contain neither `"stats"` nor `"message"`. Astra cannot reliably know its position, health, prompt state, or even most of the map. One model call per command then limits it to 6–31 actions in thirty minutes. In trace_seed1102 the first call takes 205.2 seconds, returns `move ne`, and the episode ends after six actions with score zero. Seed1101 starts with four `wait` actions and ends after eight commands.

This candidate replaces the generic serializer with complete named stats, messages, inventory with letters, numbered ASCII map, nearby monster glyph/color information and bounded message memory. It replaces one-call/one-action control with a feedback controller: shortest-path exploration, frontier discovery, gold collection, downstairs navigation, explicit search coverage, door handling, constrained food selection, recovery and single-step combat. Astra receives periodic progress summaries and exceptional situations and returns a short structured strategic plan or coordinate target. All steps are re-observed; movement failures update the graph, damage invalidates scripts, and prompts interrupt planning. Independent compact calls prevent session-history growth. Exceptions and malformed replies fall back to the controller. No System-1 or game internals are used.

The concrete experiment is whether preserving the semantic state and executing verified routine steps between strategic calls is enough to escape the zero-score plateau. The glyph correspondences used for open doorways (2371 as `.`, 2372 as `-`) are directly present in archived initial observations; movement outcomes remain authoritative. Other ambiguous glyphs are shown to Astra. No training-seed-specific rules or routes are encoded.

Confirmation: nonzero score across multiple seeds, more unique reachable positions, successful doors/stairs, and tens to hundreds of observed actions per model call without prompt loops. Refutation: controller loops, systematically attacks peaceful monsters, mishandles prompts, or loses characters before gaining score despite Astra seeing complete state. The restricted 23-action interface remains a limitation: many inventory letters and healing/equipment commands are unavailable, so the controller must not invent them.

Validation pending: pre-flight and at most two dev evaluations. Dev findings will be appended below.
