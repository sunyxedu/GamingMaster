## Hypothesis

The economic compiler undervalues waiting because it divides a fixed project prize by a fractional expected-income ETA, then executes the first affordable project even when a better project is only one favorable roll away. Real Catan income arrives in correlated integer bundles, and a seven can destroy accumulated capital. This is not a universal argument against development cards: an ore/wheat army economy can be correct. The test is to compare spending and saving in the same discrete production model.

Evidence: 0005 seed 102 buys development cards at turns 6, 14, 18 and 34, builds no ordinary settlement/city, and finishes at 3 true VP with seven ore but no wheat (trace lines 11, 23, 29, 39 and final observation). The current leader 0009 improves that outcome to 6 VP, yet buys six development cards and only one city, finishing with WOOD=0, BRICK=1, SHEEP=5, WHEAT=0, ORE=5. Its final events explicitly show RED making a maritime trade followed by `build_city 18` for the win. The leader's other loss reaches 7 VP but still has no ordinary settlement. These observations suggest a production-conversion problem that precise theft accounting alone cannot solve.

## Changes

Derive from 0005, retaining its opening, live-ID compiler, exact current-hand funding search, action postconditions and Astra recovery. Replace single-project fractional return rankings with bounded public-information dice scenarios. Each option commits to a first economic project, then evaluates a compatible second project with the added production/port and actual leftover cards. Simulated dice preserve correlations between resources on the same number; seven discards retain project-relevant cards. Waiting for a stronger first project competes with spending immediately. The executor may end the turn despite an affordable lower-ranked purchase; this is a reversible decision, not a card ban. It re-evaluates after each observation and reports failed predictions or lost targets to Astra.

The simulation is an approximation of our economy, not an engine clone: opponent purchases, future robber moves and hidden card identities are unknown. Only current bank availability and current legal IDs authorize real trades/actions. Simulated card outcomes use the documented base composition, not a hidden deck. Common synthetic dice samples compare options without touching game RNG. No seed-specific decisions or privileged state are used.

Model calls request compact strategic choices and assumptions rather than action-by-action arithmetic. Backend timeout is 300 seconds, with a 210-second foreground wait, both exceeding the recent 92-second worst inference. Exceptions and malformed replies retain the local legal fallback.

## Confirmation and refutation

Confirm through earlier ordinary city/settlement conversions on weak economies, fewer purchases that strand scarce wheat, calibration of predicted first-build times, and improved win rate with terminal true VP reported separately. Refute if waiting repeatedly misses profitable card/army paths, short-horizon production simulation favors implausible ports/routes, or forecasts fail without triggering recovery. Compare all seeds rather than fitting the two cited failures. Preflight and development evidence will be added below.

## Refinement and validation

The first preflight passed (125 controlled actions, game_done, no exception). Observation-only replay checks selected legal actions in 141 sampled archive states, including setup, robber and discard interruptions; malformed JSON, arbitrary text, stale IDs, TimeoutError, RuntimeError and missed-funding-window recovery were also checked. Forecasts took approximately 0.2 seconds in representative economic decisions. No model was queried by these checks.

First dev evaluation, seed 302: loss, 8 true/public VP, 99 completed turns, 118 controlled actions, 249 seconds, no episode error. It bought three cards, played three Knights for Army, built cities at turns 38/58/78 and an ordinary settlement at turn 74. It then waited for a two-point route while an opponent finished; 40 one-card discard actions show the cost of that wait. The one Astra request remained pending throughout play, so this run demonstrates local fallback behavior, not successful model advice. Its harness hash is recorded alongside that dev trace.

The final version therefore uses a public race horizon: eight simulated rounds while opponents are at six VP or below, six at seven, four at eight, and two at nine or above. This limits an optimistic long build chain when an opponent can finish soon. Review also corrected simulated Knight aging/one-play-per-round timing, robber suppression on the extra city production, repeated win-reward accounting, shared road-piece limits, and completion monitoring for development purchases that do not immediately change VP. Card outcome samples are shared independently of completion time. These are rule/model corrections, not fits to a seed's dice or coordinates. The documented base deck mix remains only a prior; resource cards receive a conservative option value rather than invented exact resources.

The economic leaf value uses diminishing marginal production utility plus ports; it is not an estimate of full-game win probability. Only current legal menus and exact current-hand/bank funding authorize execution. A two-build horizon can miss longer army/road paths, and even the shortened horizon does not predict the opponents' finish date. These are limitations for subsequent iterations to measure.

Final-version dev evaluation, seed 301: loss, 7 true VP / 6 public VP, 80 completed turns, 108 controlled actions, 704.5 seconds, six successful Astra calls (39.1–56.0 seconds inference), no failed calls or episode error. Astra selected the complementary opening, endorsed both city savings plans, and later switched to Army. Cities completed at turns 9 and 25; their initial predicted funding times were 2.94/3.13 own rounds versus observed 2.0/4.0. The first subsequent card forecast was optimistic (2.38 rounds predicted, 5.0 observed). It bought six development cards, secured Army, never built an ordinary settlement, and ended with ten ore stranded in a 13-card hand. Forty-one discard actions remain a serious failure signature.

Across the two development runs, win rate was **0/2**, mean terminal true VP **7.5**, composite mean **0.075**, and errors **0**. These are two different development revisions, not a final-version two-seed estimate or training result. The final version itself was tested on dev 301 only. The results do **not** support a claim of win-rate improvement. They support the narrower execution claims—explicit saving, correlated integer-income forecasts, checked conversions, and meaningful Astra interaction—but show that forecasting two local builds is insufficient to secure the full route to 10. Later iterations should investigate long-range expansion/port access, surplus liquidation before discards, and opportunity costs of abandoning a forecast midway, rather than merely increasing the city's reward.

Final preflight also passed: game_done, 125 controlled actions, no exception. It answered no System-2 requests, so it is strictly a contract check. The final archive check again passed 141 legal-action cases and malformed/stale/failed-model branches. `local_validation.json` records the final harness hash. No training/full evaluation or held-out game was run, and no other candidate was modified.
