## Hypothesis

The leader's worst losses begin before its economic compiler has a chance to work: it ranks each opening intersection independently, without pricing the scarcity of complementary sites across the snake draft. Candidate 0002 seed 102 chooses vertex 10 (`r4:a2`, trace line 4), with resource pips [0,0,5,0,5], then vertex 5 (`r10:a29`, line 6), leaving the pair at [0,4,5,2,8] in WOOD/BRICK/SHEEP/WHEAT/ORE order. This has no wood and only two wheat pips supporting eight ore pips. It buys development cards at turns 6, 14, 18, and 34, builds no ordinary settlement or city, and loses at true VP 3 after 43 completed turns. Seed 103 loses at VP 4 with no cities. The two wins make a blanket development-card ban unsafe: seed 101's early cards contribute to its award finish. Candidate 0004 already tested capital reservation and fell to one win, so this proposal tests the upstream draft rather than repeating that reservation.

**Hypothesis:** evaluate the two-settlement economy after plausible intervening opponent placements, instead of treating the highest current intersection score as the best first placement. Claiming scarce wheat first may preserve a good ore complement; choosing ore first may leave none. This should improve weak openings without disturbing the leader's successful midgame compiler.

## What changed vs. the parent

The implementation starts from 0002. A new public-observation opening planner enumerates each legal first settlement and simulates eight plausible snake drafts before choosing its best surviving second settlement. It derives the intervening player sequence from observed seat order. Opponent preferences vary between total pips, grain/ore, expansion resources, and close alternatives. They are explicitly forecasts, not a claim to reproduce AlphaBeta or access hidden information.

A pair's score combines raw production, sustainable city/development/road-plus-settlement throughput after maritime losses, useful nearby expansion sites, starting resources from the second site, and concentrated robber exposure. Throughput uses a continuous resource-flow relaxation; it is not a guarantee of affordability or an expected arrival time. First placements blend average and lower-tail performance across forecasts. The second placement is recomputed against the actual board. There are no seed IDs or memorized vertex choices in the policy.

Astra receives the ranked pair economics and possible complements, with a compact JSON reply. If all predicted complementary sites disappear, the planner reports the contradiction and `ctx.progress()` evidence for a fresh model plan. Invalid output falls back to the freshly ranked current legal menu. All actions still use current IDs; the inherited postcondition checks verify buildings, roads and trades and trigger replanning on rejection, mismatched effects, or prolonged score stagnation. The parent’s midgame projects, card policy, same-turn transaction commitment, and resource tactics are retained to isolate the opening intervention. Model timeout remains 300 seconds and foreground wait 210 seconds, both above the recent 138-second maximum; exceptions fall back to legal local execution, and only a single inference future can be pending.

## Confirmation and refutation

Primary evidence is win rate, with mean terminal true VP and worst-seed VP separately. Support would be stronger completed opening economies and earlier cities/settlements on formerly weak boards, without losing the leader's wins. Refutation would be forecasts systematically protecting the wrong complements, overvaluing theoretical maritime throughput, or favorable predicted economies still failing to convert into builds. Different openings alter subsequent random draws, so one seed's improved score alone cannot establish causality. Unseen seeds must test the draft assumptions and recovery.
