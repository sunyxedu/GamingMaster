## Hypothesis

Candidate 0003 solved the interface/latency problem, but its fixed action priorities destroy the resources needed for coherent builds. Its only training loss (seed 103, 5 VP) contains 28 maritime trades, seven paid roads, five development purchases, and **zero cities**. In `trace_seed103.jsonl`, step 111 leaves a hand of `WOOD=0, BRICK=0, SHEEP=1, WHEAT=4, ORE=2`, with five settlements and all four city pieces available. Step 112 selects `a5`, trading three wheat for brick; step 113 buys development, reducing the two saved ore to one. At steps 122–124 it turns three sheep into wheat, then three wheat into brick, and spends that brick on another road. ORANGE wins with a city at step 125. These are legal actions, but legality alone does not supply an economic objective.

The test is that a small public-state economic planner, retaining zero model latency and the leader's initial placement/robber behavior, can conserve resources for a productive building or a concrete award and raise the weak tail without losing the leader's reliable progress.

## Changes from the parent

* Replace the fixed `road > trade > development` priority with goal comparisons: each possible city, a connected settlement with up to three prerequisite roads, development purchase, and a road award reachable with at most two roads. Code forecasts resource acquisition in expected four-roll rounds, including maritime rates, then discounts the goal's VP/production value by its forecast delay. Production value declines as our true VP approaches ten.
* Interpret 2:1 and 3:1 maritime parameters correctly: the five-entry arrays have `null` padding. Verify rates against current legal trades; `self.ports` containing `null` denotes the generic port in observed traces. Only trade toward the chosen complete cost when it reduces forecast delay, advances an immediately fundable build, or reduces an exposed hand's build deficit without worsening its forecast. Recompute after each actual action; never replay an expired ID.
* Search the public road graph for distance-legal settlement sites and short award routes. Roads become investments in a specific site/award. Longest Road estimates use edge-simple trails, conservatively excluding edges incident to opponent buildings, as supported by this adaptor's public observations (see validation). Actual actions and actual award ownership remain engine controlled.
* Choose Year of Plenty against explicit build costs. Estimate Monopoly yield only from public production and opponent hand counts. Retain prompt-free forced actions and Knight play, and the parent's opening/robber policies to limit the experiment's scope.
* Remove the parent's cross-observation ID anti-repeat rule: the same ID can legitimately refer to different actions on successive revisions. Detect lack of progress from the action counter instead, and contain unexpected schema/action errors. No engine import, hypothetical engine execution, RNG use, hidden information, or model calls.

## Confirmation / refutation

Confirmation: more cities or useful expansions, fewer aimless trades/roads, faster VP accumulation and improvement on the leader's weak episodes while retaining zero call cost and no errors. Refutation: conserving for an overambitious goal delays playable development/settlement opportunities enough to lose races, or integer expected-production forecasts repeatedly wait through large hands. The new `ctx.memory['economic_goal']` is a compact audit hint; actions always resolve from the current observation. A better trade choice changes RNG consumption later, so seed-matched results are comparative evidence, not a claim of identical dice.

## Validation

* Final preflight: `ok: true`, `game_done`, 10 VP, 66 actions, zero model calls and zero fallback events.
* First permitted dev run, `dev/20261030-062728`: seeds 301/302 both won at 10 VP, in 91/77 actions. Inspection found that strictly requiring a forecast improvement deferred safe partial trades with hands above seven cards. The only economic refinement after this run allows a deficit-reducing trade at equal forecast delay when the hand is exposed to discard.
* Second and final permitted dev run, `dev/20261030-063113`: seeds 301/302 both won at 10 VP, in 64/66 actions, with no errors, fallbacks, or model calls. Both built four cities. Initial observations differ between the two dev invocations, so the reduced action counts are **not** an isolated causal estimate of the refinement. No full training evaluation or held-out evaluation was run.
* Offline archive checks: the planner returned a current legal ID without exceptions on every nonterminal observation from all eight parent training traces. All 226 observed maritime transitions matched the null-aware resource arithmetic. The observed paid costs matched the implemented costs: 76 roads, 64 development purchases, 14 settlements, and 16 cities; ten extra roads were free.
* A rules-verification check found that ordinary edge-trail counting overestimates some public road lengths by one. Example: seed 105, step 137 has RED's chain `40–18–16–21–43–44`, but WHITE owns vertex 40 and the engine reports length four, not five. Excluding edges incident to an opponent building matches every public global maximum in the 1,203 parent observations except 89 initialization-era observations whose recorded award length is still zero. This conservative correction was applied after the final dev run; replaying all 130 final-dev decisions with the corrected code changed **zero** selected IDs, and final preflight passed afterward. It is an observation-supported model of this build, not a claim about tabletop rules.

Remaining limitations: expected-production waiting times are heuristics, not stochastic rollouts; they omit future bank shortages and robber/award retaliation. The opening and robber policy deliberately retain the parent's weaknesses. Development value is an approximate prior rather than a reconstructed hidden deck. Current legal actions and observed outcomes remain authoritative.
