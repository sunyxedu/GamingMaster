Hypothesis: the current plateau is a resource ceiling, not just a failure to merge. Candidate 0009 completes all 200 moves and consolidates well, but its worst seed 37 ends with total tile mass 438, merge score 2672, max tile 256, combined 2928. Its final board is [[256,128,32,0],[16,0,0,0],[0,0,2,0],[4,0,0,0]] (trace_seed37.jsonl, episode_end). No rearrangement of 438 mass can make 512. Its other tied-low seeds 59/83 have mass 444/446. Every training episode has max_tile 256. The seed-37 forecast stays at combined 2928 from move 100 through 200 (`verified_trajectory_step`), consistent with a plateau in its proposals. This experiment asks whether acquiring extra spawned value can break that ceiling.

This is derived from 0003's verified seed-stream simulator, not a refinement of 0009's terminal-trajectory mutation recipe. Different actions change the number of empty cells used by rejection-sampled random choice, so they can change the subsequent random cursor and 2/4 draw. The stream is a public, generic hypothesis checked against the opening and every actual action; there are no seed lists or memorized paths. An occupancy-stratified beam preserves different empty-cell counts and, within each class, distinct RNG cursors and resource totals. This retains the denser setup paths that alter rejection sampling instead of allowing the shape heuristic to eliminate them immediately. It seeks additional 4s while preserving mergeability, then switches to consolidation when guaranteed future 2s supply the target resource budget. Short verified action blocks preserve the discovered setup before another search. Leaf bonuses fade to actual merge score plus max tile at the move cap. The key new variable is resource acquisition: merges conserve mass; spawn value is the only source of new mass.

Astra receives the exact board, mass/four counts, predicted acquisition, recent checked transitions, and ctx.progress evidence. It can request acquisition, consolidation, or space repair for a bounded interval. Reviews run asynchronously, use 180-second timeouts (above the observed 105-second recent maximum), and catch failures and arbitrary output. Search has a four-second decision limit, checks ctx.done within each expansion layer, and accepts only complete layers. It commits at most four predicted actions, each independently checked before retaining the suffix. Every step verifies slide, score, move counter, spawn metadata, board, and mass conservation. A seed discrepancy permanently removes resource-aware deterministic planning and invokes all-spawn expectation; a rule discrepancy suspends search for observed-legal probes and asks Astra to diagnose it. Two matching probes are needed to restore local rules. Stagnation, rejected actions, missing acquisition, and collapsed space trigger re-planning.

Evidence distinguishing the hypothesis: increased final mass and observed 4-spawn counts, followed by 512 creation and higher combined score, supports it. More mass without better score refutes the assumption that local consolidation can use it. Unchanged mass means the resource search cannot find useful cursor divergences. Early death or missed move cap means acquisition destroys layout or consumes too much compute. Replay disagreements falsify the RNG assumption and must visibly disable it. The mass effect should generalize to unseen seeds under the same verified public RNG semantics; it cannot be assumed under an unrelated RNG. The default 200-move cap is documented, and beyond it the controller resumes a rolling horizon.

Archive reliability evidence: 0003 seed 97 reaches move 123 with valid rule/replay checks, asks for a collapsed-space review at clock 834.69, and remains blocked until the 1800-second deadline. This harness never waits for an optional model review before continuing verified play.

Validation: required preflight passed and completed 200 actions with arbitrary stand-in models. The exact simulator and public RNG hypothesis matched all 1,600 archived leader transitions; explicit merge cases include [2,2,4,0] -> [4,4,0,0], correcting a study-document typo. Fault injection exercised an action rejection, transition disagreement, model TimeoutError, and malformed JSON replies without an escaping exception.

Two allowed dev evaluations were used. The first, a narrower beam on seed 5, scored 3056 in 200 moves with mass 478 and 37 spawned 4s. Its leaf boards mostly had 9–11 empties, motivating occupancy/cursor stratification. The final structural version on dev seeds 5/7 scored 3052/3056 (mean 3054), finished both episodes in about 199 seconds, and recorded no rule or seed mismatches. Final mass was 480/484, with 38/40 spawned 4s; both still had maximum tile 256. Each requested one asynchronous review, but neither had an answered model call before episode end. This validates liveness and shows high acquired mass, but does NOT establish the 512 hypothesis or a causal improvement over the leader on the same seeds. No training evaluation was run. A final arithmetic fix makes the acquisition bonus vanish at any leaf on move 200, including a search started more than 24 moves earlier; preflight was rerun after that fix.
