Hypothesis: candidate 0002's stochastic expectimax is sound, but its Astra-selected mode never changes the geometric plan. When a bad spawn damages the chosen snake, the search continues to score moves against the same implicit orientation and can spend many turns oscillating without restoring a corner. Candidate 0003 shows that a much deeper replay tree is too expensive: on seed 83 it reaches the deadline while space collapses. A bounded controller that re-estimates the corner orientation from the observed board, switches risk weights only at evidence-driven reviews, and uses shallow chance search should recover damaged layouts while retaining most of the 200-move budget.

What changed vs. the parent: this harness starts from 0002's exact local transition and spawn-agnostic expectimax, but adds four rotation-invariant snake orders and an `infer_orientation` step before every move. An occupied maximum corner is treated as strong evidence; otherwise the best monotone order is selected, with hysteresis to avoid needless flipping. Astra now selects orientation, risk (`space`, `merge`, or `balanced`), and depth 1–3 at opening, stagnation, verification disagreement, rejected action, and periodic anchor-health checks. Search depth is capped at two while the board is sparse and runs for a short local deadline, avoiding 0003's deep replay timeout. Every realized move checks slide result, merge score, move count, and the single spawn difference; `ctx.progress()` triggers replanning after scoreless or unchanged stretches.

Evidence that would confirm or refute it: confirmation would show fewer long scoreless stretches than 0002, more consistent maximum tiles on weak seeds, and no deadline episodes despite at most five short Astra reviews. Review logs should show orientation changes after a damaged corner and subsequent score progress. Refutation would be unchanged orientation throughout, repeated oscillation after full-board pressure, or lower merge score despite similar move counts; those outcomes would indicate that orientation inference is too aggressive or that a deeper, seed-independent search is needed.
