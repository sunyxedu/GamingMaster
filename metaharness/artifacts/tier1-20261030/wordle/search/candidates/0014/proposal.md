Hypothesis: the flat scores are partly caused by committing to a fixed first guess or an expensive full tree. A public-list adaptive opener can choose a probe suited to the current answer snapshot, while exact candidate-only tails are enough once feedback has reduced the state. The key risk is computational: a theoretically better opener is useless if its lookahead exhausts the wall budget before it is posted.

What changed versus parent 0008:

- The opener is selected from the public legal list. Letter-frequency coverage and a cheap partition score screen the pool, then only the strongest dozen receive a two-step attainable reward estimate. This prevents the opener search from stopping on the first alphabetical word when the wall guard fires.
- The tail is recomputed from the complete accepted history after every action. For states of at most 30 answers, a memoized exact candidate-only Bellman search chooses the next guess; larger states use an attainable sequential-tail reward and balanced partitions. This explicitly handles the penultimate turn, where a probe can still leave a final answer.
- Duplicate-aware green-first feedback, public-file hashes, strict history parsing, legal/repeat checks, and post-action verification remain in code. `ctx.progress()` rejection and stale-observation evidence trigger a bounded Astra recovery prompt. Recovery output is parsed as one complete unused legal word and falls back only to a consistent candidate.
- No seed, answer, or training trace enters ranking. The only model call is recovery after evidence that the scripted plan was not accepted or its predicted partition was contradicted.

Evidence: the leader 0012's seed 0 trace spends about 120 seconds on a tail search, then solves `slate -> doric -> bumfs -> rerun` in four guesses; its all-gray and yellow-t compatible distributions reduce late solves but lower total reward. Candidate 0008 already showed that opener selection is a distinct structural variable, while 0009/0011 showed that exhaustive proof does not guarantee a better finite sample. The first dev run of this candidate exposed an efficiency failure: deep lookahead over every opener timed out after choosing `aback`, and both games took four guesses. After cheap screening, the second dev run chose `slate`; it solved one seed in three guesses but the repeated-letter seed still took five (`slate -> tonic -> turbo -> tutor -> tumor`). The exact small-tail addition addresses that concrete failure mode. These dev results are diagnostic and do not claim a score improvement.

Evidence that would confirm the hypothesis is a higher training and unseen mean with no errors, together with opener logs showing broad public-list adaptation and exact-tail choices reducing four/five-guess branches. Refutation would be unchanged or lower mean despite complete opener scans, or repeated-letter states still requiring late guesses after exact-tail search. The main limitation is that large states use a shallow attainable heuristic rather than a globally proved tree; logs distinguish that path from exact tails.

Validation: preflight passes with arbitrary stand-in inference, four accepted actions, and no errors. Two allowed dev invocations completed without errors or model calls; the first mean was 0.50 with four guesses on both seeds, and the second mean was 0.50 (three and five guesses) after the opener-screening fix. No full training, validation, or held-out result was used.
