Hypothesis: the remaining plateau may reflect the risk preference of the decision tree, rather than a wrong feedback rule or insufficient model calls. A mean-reward policy can trade a few difficult five/six-guess outcomes for many easy solves. The seed groups are stratified by repeated-letter status, so protecting difficult feedback families is worth testing as a separate objective. This is an explicitly risk-averse experiment: improving the worst outcomes can lower the mean score that selects the winner. Unlike this iteration's search-allocation and rollout proposals, this candidate changes the policy objective itself.

Archive evidence: the leader 0004 is error-free with a 0.50 minimum and 0.611 mean. Its actual seed-0 trace records `candidates:165, guess:diner, total_reward:528, search_finished:false`, then `heron -> rerun`, solving in four guesses. Seed 3 records a finished search at 61 candidates choosing `weber`, followed by a nine-candidate `colin` probe and then `helix`, again four guesses. Seed 5 is `slate -> cornu -> guise -> issue`. Those are accepted actions and consistent deductions; they do not prove an incorrect guess. Candidate 0009 spends more work proving expected-reward decisions but still averages 0.583 on training and dev. This motivates a different tree objective rather than another opener metric or proof-budget adjustment. Some earlier proposals misquote the leader's seed-0 path as `slate -> decor -> bairn -> rerun`; that path belongs to 0003, not 0004.

Changes versus parent 0004:

- Retain `slate`, the uniform public-answer universe, exact green-first duplicate-aware filtering from the entire accepted history, public-file hash checks, feedback parser, and common action verifier. No hidden answer, seed, frequency corpus, or prior episode enters a decision.
- Replace scalar expected-reward maximization with a distributional policy-tree objective. For a state with r turns left, H[k] counts answers receiving k remaining-turn reward units (k=0 is failure). Minimize H lexicographically: first failures, then last-turn solves, then next-to-last-turn solves, through earliest solves. Child histograms add, so this criterion is consistent across branches. This takes the minimax motivation of 0003 beyond a one-step bucket statistic, but implements it by replacing 0004's Bellman values, bounds, pruning, and incumbents with histograms.
- Search all unused legal words for distinct feedback partitions. Exact singleton/pair and two-turn solutions, partition deduplication, and optimistic histogram bounds reduce work. The optimistic bound solves one answer now and every other answer next turn; summing those child bounds permits safe lexicographic pruning. A sequential candidate policy is an attainable incumbent even if interrupted.
- Search small states without a beam. Larger states retain a diversified beam chosen by hardest bucket, optimistic histogram, collision count, and candidate guesses. Thus large-state results are approximate; logs distinguish finishing the beam from an exact result. A 120-second decision guard returns the best fully evaluated tree and checks both ctx.done and remaining wall time. The logged reward histogram exposes predicted failures and late solves, alongside its implied total reward.
- Execute one action at a time. Verify turn advancement, exact history append, predicted feedback membership, candidate reduction, error fields, and ctx.progress() rejection/staleness counters. Every recovery action passes the same checks. Contradictions go to Astra with the full observation, evidence, progress, and surviving candidates; a bounded call catches timeouts and runtime failures, validates one complete legal unused word, and falls back to a consistent candidate after garbage output.

Confirmation would be fewer losses or late solves and a higher weakest-seed score, ideally without sacrificing mean reward. Compare logged histograms and conditional mean score to distinguish tail protection from lucky answers. Refutation would be lower mean with no improved worst outcomes, or excessive probing that shifts many three-guess solves to four. Exact small-state tests establish correctness of the new objective, not that it is optimal for the evaluator's mean-score criterion. Unseen evaluation remains necessary.
