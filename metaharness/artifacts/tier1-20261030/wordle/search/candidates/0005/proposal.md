Hypothesis: one-step worst-partition minimization spends guesses on information that does not maximize the game's actual expected score. Candidate 0003 is reliable (12/12 solves, mean 3.50 guesses, minimum score 0.50), but its objective ignores both immediate wins and the future cost of each feedback branch. This candidate tests a different planning objective: expected-case opening/midgame partitions followed by finite-horizon reward optimization in the endgame.

Archive evidence: `0003/trace_seed0.jsonl` records `guess slate` → `guess decor` → `guess bairn` → `guess rerun`, ending at score 0.50. After `decor`, its feedback is `[gray,green,gray,gray,yellow]`; the next non-answer probe `bairn` spends another turn before the solve. `0003/trace_seed3.jsonl` likewise records `slate` → `lower` → `belly` → `helix` at 0.50. These traces establish extra guesses, not that any particular replacement is superior. For comparison, candidate 0002's worst trace, `trace_seed43.jsonl`, guesses `raise` → `basic` → `basil` → `basin` → `basis` (score 0.3333), demonstrating the separate risk of refusing useful probes. The proposal therefore retains legal non-answer probes while pricing their eventual solve cost.

What changed: early guesses minimize expected residual candidate count, sum of squared feedback-bin sizes divided by candidate count; worst-bin size is only a tie-break. The opener is recomputed from the public lists over all answers plus 500 letter-coverage-ranked legal probes. Later heuristic turns retain every surviving answer and up to 1,200 ranked probes, rather than the parent's alphabetically truncated probe pool. The public uniform answer distribution supplies the prior; no seed identities, observed final answers, or memorized per-seed plans are used.

The structural change is the endgame planner. For at most ten surviving answers it considers every unused legal guess, merges equivalent feedback partitions, memoizes candidate subsets, and maximizes the sum of future reward numerators. With t turns remaining, an immediate win contributes t, a non-green branch contributes its recursively optimized value at t-1, and an unsolved answer contributes zero. This directly represents the evaluator's score after dividing by six and the candidate count. A non-answer probe remains eligible even on the penultimate turn when its additional distinguishable outcomes outweigh the foregone immediate win. On the last turn the planner guesses an answer. Search is exact when it completes; a 45-second bound and deadline checks fall back to the heuristic. The two-turn heuristic also counts attainable reward rather than entropy.

Execution and recovery: after every action the harness checks progress counters, accepted-turn advancement, the appended history row, and whether the observed feedback belongs to a predicted branch. Rejections, unchanged observations, malformed history, empty candidate sets, and action exceptions trigger an Astra recovery prompt with the observation and evidence. Accepted guesses and rejected words are excluded from later actions. Recovery output must contain a whole legal five-letter word; garbage, TimeoutError, and backend RuntimeError fall back safely. Calls use 220-second timeouts, above the archive's 176-second slowest call. Repeated failures are bounded. Every accepted clue causes full-history filtering again, including after recovery.

Confirming evidence would be higher mean score without new losses or episode errors, especially fewer four/five-guess branches, and endgame trace events showing reward-based choices. Lower mean score or more losses would refute the policy hypothesis even if partition statistics improve. Excessive compute or frequent bounded-search fallback would show the endgame threshold is impractical. The model is reserved for demonstrated plan failures, so normal episodes should continue to need zero System-2 calls.

Validation so far: the initial expected-case policy passed preflight and solved dev seeds 1001 and 1002 in three guesses each (mean 0.6667), with no model calls or errors. After adding exact endgame search and stronger recovery, preflight passed again. Duplicate-aware feedback matched an independent implementation on 361 answer/guess pairs. Reward-optimal choices matched an independent brute-force decision-tree solver on 70 small problems with one to four turns remaining. Six synthetic control-loop checks covered normal execution, rejected actions, unchanged observations, action exceptions, recovery timeouts, and garbage model output; all completed without escaping exceptions. Dev results are only a smoke test, not evidence of superiority on the training or unseen distributions.

Final dev evaluation (`dev/20261031-082822`): 2/2 solves, three guesses each, mean score 0.6666667, zero System-2 calls, no errors, about 115 seconds per episode. Seed 1001 exercised exact endgame search with three candidates, four turns remaining, and total reward numerator 10; it selected `bongo`. The final trajectories were `roate` → `godly` → `bongo` and `roate` → `trout` → `tumor`. These outputs were used for verification only; there are no seed-specific branches or words inserted into the policy. Most runtime is the cold opening calculation, a deliberate cost of selecting the opener from the public distribution. Two dev evaluations were completed; no full training evaluation was run.
