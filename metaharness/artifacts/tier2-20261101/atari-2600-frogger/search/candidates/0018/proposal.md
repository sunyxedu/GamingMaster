# 0018 — Contact-triggered escape shield with rider-specific motion

**Parent:** 0014 (rewrite), retaining 0017's evaluated route portfolio and its independent auxiliary/median hazard layers. This is a structural change to who handles the last, lethal part of a crossing: route search proposes a useful itinerary, but a separate frame-local shield now owns emergency movement whenever an observed contact alert or a carried-frog edge trajectory makes waiting unsafe. Astra reviews *failure classes and policy*, Jev retains durable crossing advice; neither model is on the emergency-action path.

## Hypothesis and trace evidence

The best candidate, 0017, raised the mean to 4433 and 53 homes, but its floor is repeated short-range river losses, not lack of route throughput. In the actual lowest-scoring episode (seed127, 3500 points), the trace records:

* Tick **16280**: frog row8/x100, on a shape-184 turtle, `hazard_e7=128`, auxiliary candidate row8/x108, and the last plan is a `NOOP` from tick16265 with predicted margin3.5. The frog is only 8 world pixels from the actor when the life is lost.
* Tick **22094**: row8/x148 with the same-row actor x132; the preceding plan at tick22074 selected RIGHT at x131. Edge rejection at x148 is too late to protect an already carried frog.
* Tick **26514** repeats the row8/x100, auxiliary x108, hazard-bit witness. Tick **28496** is a distinct row7/x70 failure with timer1, showing that an emergency escape cannot turn into an unlimited wait.

This is not just a weak geometric margin. The parent already screens the candidate actor, yet an action can become stale while the frog is being carried and the fatal event occurs between planning opportunities. 0014 also recorded a bank/road loss at row5/x32 after a long plan, and five final/river gate failures in its worst seed101 despite margins0–1. The repeated row8 witnesses in 0017 (including seed113 ticks22991/25021/36327/38245/38881, all frog x100 with the candidate actor on row8 and `hazard_e7=128`) motivate a distinct fast safety layer rather than another global clearance pad.

**Testable hypothesis:** a same-row contact alert should cause an immediate, geometry-checked vertical escape when possible, rather than another dwell or lateral adjustment on the actor's lane. Separately, estimating the *specific carrier under the frog* instead of averaging every carrier in the row should make edge corrections early enough to matter. If these two changes work, repeated x100/actor-near losses and x148+ edge losses should fall without reducing complete-home throughput or causing long stalls.

## What changes

1. **Keep the route portfolio:** 0017's time-expanded, copy-aware search, through-bank launch, itinerary portfolio, neutral press/release, matched home-alert veto, auxiliary-track screen, and higher-level median-track check remain the strategic baseline. This preserves the demonstrated 53-home throughput rather than replacing it with a slower reactive controller.
2. **Carrier-specific motion estimate:** after updating per-object velocity from successive public positions, identify the safe carrier whose collision interval actually contains the frog. Use that carrier's measured velocity for the current row's drift forecast (future rows keep the row estimate). Do not average the other, potentially separated carrier into the rider's motion. The estimate is episode-local and is reset naturally by the row/epoch transition.
3. **Fast contact escape shield:** at each fresh game observation—including frames inside a held pulse—if E7's high hazard bit is set, the decoded auxiliary candidate is on the frog's row, and their world positions are within 32 pixels, the shield considers only currently legal, live-geometry-checked one-hop actions. It first prefers UP or DOWN to leave the actor's row; if neither is supported, it picks a safe lateral hop that increases separation. If the chosen escape differs from a direction currently held, code posts one NOOP release frame and recomputes the escape from the next observation. It never commits an unchecked model answer, and an unavailable escape falls back to the ordinary latest route/guard rather than inventing safety. The shield records which action it overrides and why.
4. **Early carried-edge correction:** when the frog is marked as carried and its matched carrier's measured drift projects it toward a world edge over the next two motor intervals, check an opposite lateral correction and vertical exits before the normal route action. This intervenes before the old x148 hard bound rather than attempting to recover at x152.
5. **Model division:** code handles contact geometry, measured motion, legal alternatives, and emergency motor control. Jev continues to choose durable itinerary/protocol categories from named facts and additionally gives a one-decision typed preference among code-generated first-hop candidates; its response is eligible only when a fresh route still offers that action at comparable route value and the latest guard accepts it. The hard contact/edge shield has priority over Jev. Astra receives recent concrete failure classes asynchronously and can revise bounded enums only; it cannot disable the safety shield.

## System-1 state, questions, and answer bridge

The request state is the parent's compact strict JSON: phase, current crossing commitment, open-home records, up to one code-generated route candidate per immediate action (with completeness, endpoint, margin bucket and goal), policy, timer urgency, recent failure category, auxiliary actor row/presence, matched-home alert, bank-risk facts, and whether a complete launch exists. It contains derived names rather than raw RAM, no arithmetic task, and no seed-specific facts. Questions are batched: two order-reversed Choices for whole-crossing bay itinerary and TRANSIT/STAGE/RECOVER/SCREEN, another reversed Choice pair over the legal first-hop candidates plus KEEP_CODE, and a Noul for whether explicit repeated-failure/bank evidence warrants an Astra review.

Each order-reversed Choice pair (itinerary, protocol, and motor preference) must agree; the selected option's probability in **each** answer must be at least **0.70**. A result is accepted only for the same death/home/level epoch and policy version, with age at most **90 ticks** for durable advice. The motor preference has a tighter **24-tick** freshness limit and is consumed once; at the next action opportunity it must still match a current route with the same complete/partial status and value within five points of code's best route. Missing/malformed answers, disagreement, timeout, low probability, or a vanished candidate preserve the code route. A Jev response cannot suppress an E7/near-actor escape or override the live collision/support check. Astra is called only when failure evidence justifies review; valid bounded enum output changes future routing preference and invalidates old Jev answers. Its ordinary timeout or malformed JSON preserves the prior policy.

## Timing and fallback

Jev remains asynchronous with a 3s timeout (comfortably above the measured normal p90 and the observed tail); no motor path waits for it. Astra remains asynchronous with a 120s timeout and is rate-limited to avoid consuming the episode on review calls. Directions are pulsed and explicitly rearmed with NOOP as in 0017. At every game tick the route is rechecked against the current observation; emergency movement uses the same legal-action guard. If the shield cannot establish a safe exit, the existing route/guard fallback runs, then NOOP only if no legal route is available. The controller continues until game completion or the episode context closes.

## Confirmation and refutation

Confirm if the 0017-like repeated row8/x100 + same-row actor + E7 witness count drops, x148+ carried-edge failures disappear or move earlier to successful corrections, and the mean homes/score stays at or above the 0017 baseline. Log shield triggers, selected escape, verified movement, and subsequent life outcome so an override is not mistaken for a successful save. Refute if alerts frequently trigger on harmless actors and drain timer, if the matched-carrier estimate is noisier than row-average drift, if the shield cannot find supported exits and stalls, or if the extra supervision reduces home throughput without reducing deaths.

No held-out seed or game-specific source was used. Results are not claimed before evaluation. A 15-second live training-seed smoke test on seed127 ended at the trial cap with score152, 2 homes, 0 deaths, and no error; the trace showed three shield overrides, including one press interruption followed by a recomputed escape. This is evidence that the new branch ran, not a score comparison or confirmation of safety.
