## Hypothesis

The portfolio compiler ranks investments using fractional expected resources, while the game requires integer cards and complete maritime exchange lots. It also immediately buys any affordable project even if waiting would complete a much stronger investment. This makes its ETA optimistic on scarce-resource economies and fails to price the opportunity cost of cheap spending. Candidate 0012 tests a discrete stochastic investment scheduler, including waiting as a real choice, rather than adding another reservation rule or improving the leader's information ledger.

Concrete evidence from the current leader: `0009/trace_seed103.jsonl` line 11 presents `dev` with ETA 0 but `ready:false`: the hand is WOOD 0, BRICK 1, SHEEP 0, WHEAT 1, ORE 4, so one spare brick plus three spare ore create one fractional trade credit but cannot buy the missing sheep at 4:1; line 18 estimates `city:1` at two rounds at turn 11. That city is not built until turn 39 (line 49), with a development purchase at turn 23 and a road at turn 27 in between. The episode loses at 7 true VP. Seed 102 spends four ORE for WHEAT and immediately buys a development card at turn 30 (lines 42–43), despite the value of ore toward a city, and loses at 6 VP. These are symptoms, not proof: development cards also supply Largest Army, and the dice may simply be unlucky. The experiment must preserve viable card paths.

## Change from parent

Retain 0002's public graph reconstruction, opening policy, exact live maritime compiler, tactical cards, legality checks, and postcondition recovery. Replace its continuous midgame project ranking with a two-investment scenario scheduler. Evaluate up to 14 alternative first investments on 28 common dice worlds spanning ten personal rounds. Resource yields sharing the same number arrive together, maritime trades consume whole lots, and sevens discard half of large hands. After completing the first investment, choose a second using only the hand already realized in that world, then score its actual sampled completion. Account for added city/settlement production, new ports, shared paid access roads, and remaining pieces. Keep the parent's strategic project values so the experiment changes timing and opportunity-cost estimation rather than introducing a new VP valuation. Live repeated discards preserve one goal and use the same surplus policy as the forecast. Waiting is allowed only when the forecast says the delayed investment sequence beats currently funded alternatives. Refresh all plans after every observed action; never execute simulated actions directly. Waiting requires at least 50% sampled completion within two rounds and a margin over immediately funded alternatives; it is disabled when an opponent has nine public VP. A waiting deadline spans at most four personal rounds and survives changes of target. Completed purchases terminate their transaction, including development cards, so a repeated card purchase must compete afresh.

The discrete forecast is explicitly an approximation of future economics, not a reconstructed hidden game. Astra receives projected completion probabilities, delay/competition evidence, and the persistent investment intent, and can revise strategic mode or choose a current legal recovery action. Forecast misses, lost targets, rejected actions, or postcondition contradictions trigger replanning through `ctx.progress()`. Model calls use the parent's 300-second timeout and bounded foreground wait, above the recent observed 92-second maximum. Garbage output and failed calls retain legal fallback execution. If new failure evidence appears while an obsolete call is still queued, retire its answer and submit a fresh replanning request, with at most two outstanding model calls. Completed retired answers are ignored; the context owns cancellation at episode end. This fixes a recovery defect observed during the first dev run: an unresolved prior failure could clear a new waiting deadline repeatedly, and the only queued prompt had no knowledge of that failure.

## Confirmation / refutation

Confirm with fewer ETA-zero/unfundable predictions, successful conversion of saved cards into timely cities/settlements, no prolonged waiting beyond forecast limits, and improved win rate and weak-seed true VP. Refute if sampling misranks rare-resource economies, discards erase savings, waiting cedes expansion sites, or the two-investment horizon suppresses effective Army/VP paths. Report win rate and mean terminal true VP separately. No training-seed decisions, hidden information, game-source imports, or held-out data are used.

Limitations: forecasts do not simulate opponent purchases, theft, contested-site occupancy, new hostile robber targeting, or future bank shortages. Current funding is exact; future bank liquidity is an assumption. The current robber block is removed probabilistically after a sampled seven. Development outcomes retain heuristic project value rather than sampling a hidden deck. The finite horizon and small common sample set can misrank close choices. Astra sees these assumptions and failure evidence; live legality and observed postconditions override every forecast.

Pre-flight repair: the loop lacked a real-wall-clock admission reserve before synchronous opponent advances, allowing a game call to outlive the 60-second deadline; added a 45-second/adaptive step reserve, cooperative search cancellation, immediate budget-exception unwinding, and defensive non-text reply parsing while retaining the investment scheduler.
