## Hypothesis

The economic compiler treats opponent resources as almost entirely unknown, even though the observation exposes enough information to recover their combined holdings exactly. This wastes tactical opportunities and weakens robber targeting. This experiment adds a persistent public-information resource ledger and evaluates development-card uses by their immediate economic consequences. It tests information processing and tactical timing, independently of the parallel capital-allocation experiments.

Concrete archive evidence: in `0002/trace_seed102.jsonl`, controlled step 18 (`r64:a1`) played Monopoly on BRICK. The preceding observation gives opponents' aggregate holdings as WOOD 0, BRICK 4, SHEEP 6, WHEAT 2, ORE 4 using `19 - bank - own_hand`. The inherited policy instead estimates holdings from smoothed production shares. This does not prove SHEEP was the best economic choice, but the choice was made without using observable exact yields. Earlier, at step 12, the agent ended its turn with Monopoly playable and 4 sheep available from opponents. Seed 102 ultimately finished at 3 true VP, with final public events `maritime_trade` BRICK→WHEAT then `build_settlement 47` by RED. Seed 103 finished at 4 true VP; WHITE won via a WHEAT→SHEEP trade then `build_settlement 39`. Both losses show why the resource bottleneck and its immediate conversion matter, beyond a tile's raw pip count.

I checked the conservation identity against every observation in the two worst episodes of both 0002 and 0005: all 206 observed states had nonnegative resource totals whose sum matched the opponents' public hand counts. The harness repeats this check at runtime rather than assuming the identity forever.

## Confirmation and refutation

Confirm with accurate Monopoly postconditions, resource-card uses that unlock builds in the same turn, fewer robber choices that merely maximize pips while leaving the leader's bottleneck untouched, and improved win rate/minimum true VP on weak boards. Refute if uncertain individual estimates are systematically misleading, conservation fails, resource cards are spent without conversions, or the altered tactics do not improve the race. Public aggregate totals do not identify individual hidden hands, so those estimates remain an explicit limitation. All rules are inferred from public observations; there are no seed IDs, hidden hands, engine imports, or deck reads.
