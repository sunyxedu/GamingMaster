## Hypothesis

The leader's tactical resource ledger raises weak-seed VP, but its additive opening score still admits economies that cannot grow fast enough. An opening should be evaluated by **discrete time to compound production**, including correlated dice, integer maritime exchanges, and seven-discards, rather than pips or fractional trade throughput alone. This candidate tests that structural opening evaluator while keeping 0009's midgame projects, resource ledger, card tactics and action verification.

Concrete evidence: in `0009/trace_seed102.jsonl`, steps 1/3 choose vertices 10/5, totaling resource pips `[0,4,5,2,8]` in WOOD/BRICK/SHEEP/WHEAT/ORE order. Step 12 uses BRICK Monopoly and step 14 builds city 10, so the tactical improvement works. But no ordinary settlement is ever built; at step 62 it loses at 6 true VP with only those two sites. Steps 17, 35, 41 and 47 trade four SHEEP or ORE for one WHEAT and immediately buy development cards. Its final hand is WOOD 0, BRICK 1, SHEEP 5, WHEAT 0, ORE 5. In seed 103, sites 1/10 become cities at steps 10 and 35, but there is again no ordinary settlement before losing at 7 VP. This does not prove a different opening wins, but it identifies a production-growth failure rather than an illegal-action loop.

Candidate 0007 already showed that paired draft evaluation can change which boards win (two wins, different seeds). Its dev 302 result also refutes treating continuous maritime throughput as a sufficient opening metric: it selected a pair with no ore or brick, bought ten development cards, built no city and lost at 6 VP. This proposal carries forward its varied public snake-draft forecasts, replacing that relaxation with discrete resource trajectories and pairing them with the stronger 0009 tactics.

## Changes

* Enumerate legal first placements and eight plausible public opponent drafts. At the actual second placement, discard the forecast board and rank the actual live legal choices. Mean and lower-tail draft performance determine first-site rank. Expansion forecasts exclude settlements occupied or distance-blocked by that simulated draft; cached rollout economics refresh their route details for each scenario.
* For each pair, simulate 20 common, independent dice trajectories over twelve own rounds. Dice produce integer resource bundles at their actual numbers, including same-number correlations. Own buying opportunities are approximated in blocks of four rolls. Only the second site supplies the initial hand. Trades spend integer port rates while preserving the entire purchase recipe; sevens discard half a hand over seven. No game seed, hidden hand, engine or deck is read.
* Compare six city/expansion investment orders. Cities increase subsequent production; a reachable third site adds its production and port. The observed first road is respected during second-site evaluation, and one free setup road is allowed when it has not yet been placed. Utility rewards earlier completed growth and lower-tail outcomes, with a small raw-production tie-break. This is a forecast, not a compiled action sequence.
* Astra sees the ranked options, assumptions, expected growth timings and the parent resource/race context. It may override the ranking with a current legal ID. Lost predicted complements and missed first-growth or third-site milestones trigger replanning with `ctx.progress()` evidence. A censored forecast (fewer than 75% of paths finish within twelve rounds) is labeled as an incomplete horizon rather than a precise failed prediction. The setup road follows the chosen expansion direction when its first edge is currently legal. All action legality, rejection and postcondition checks remain active.
* Retain 300 s model timeout and 210 s foreground bound, both above the archive's recent 92 s worst observed inference. Exceptions and garbage output fall back to the current legal portfolio. A delayed answer cannot apply its old action ID to a new revision. If an execution/forecast failure occurs while an older revision's request is still pending, issue a fresh evidence-bearing replan request, retaining at most one retired request plus one active request. This is bounded to avoid letting queued opening inference suppress all later recovery; both tasks are managed and cancelled by ctx.

## Confirmation / refutation

Support would be faster first production growth and third settlements, improved weak-board VP and win rate, and useful replan events when forecasts fail. Refute if favorable rollout scores fail to produce growth, alternate drafts lose strong economies, or midgame spending dominates outcomes regardless of opening. Compare primary win rate and mean terminal true VP separately. These small samples do not establish causality, and changing actions changes later random draws.

The simulator omits future opponents' road blocking, theft, bank shortages and robber movement. It is explicitly optimistic and does not predict exact dice or hidden cards. Those limitations motivate lower-tail scoring, fresh second-site ranking, live legality, and forecast-failure feedback to Astra. Midgame capital-allocation policy is intentionally inherited, so this trial isolates an opening intervention.

## Validation

Final preflight passed after the stale-request recovery change: no exception, normal deadline termination after 79 controlled actions. The focused recovery and legality checks also passed. The first prototype also passed and completed its smoke episode; neither stand-in preflight answered System-2, so their VP is not performance evidence. Separate checks covered 348 normal-play observations from all four parent training traces: every proposed local action belonged to its current legal menu. Garbage prose, malformed JSON, stale IDs, TimeoutError and RuntimeError recovered safely; focused checks verified integer trade thresholds, deadline-stop behavior, and separate first-growth/third-site failure evidence even when army or hidden VP inflate score. Artifacts: `mechanics_checks.json`, `preflight_result.json`, `archive_opening_checks.txt`.

Public archive opening calculations took 19–59 seconds for the first draft and under one second for actual second-placement menus. These are counterfactual ranking checks, not replayed game scores. Ranking retains the parent's first site on the seed-101 board and changes it on the other three boards. None of these observations was used to tune weights or select a seed-specific action.

Dev seed 301 won: win rate 1/1, terminal true/public VP 10/10, 107 controlled actions, 77 completed normal turns, 287 seconds, no errors. It chose sites 4/13; built cities on turns 9 and 17, settlements on turns 45 and 73, and the winning city on turn 77. Its one Astra request did not return before the game ended, so this validates local execution under admission delay, not model guidance. After this run, final changes added explicit third-site milestone monitoring, refreshed cached scenario route details, and an early deadline stop; the economic scores and action compiler were unchanged. Those changes passed the focused checks and final preflight.

Dev seed 302 lost at 9 true VP / 6 public VP: 156 controlled actions, 149 completed normal turns, 390 seconds, two System-2 requests, one answered, no errors. Astra selected the local first-ranked site 28 with a WOOD port; fallback selected site 15. The pair's pips were `[4,0,0,5,6]`. It built cities at turns 10 and 50 but no third settlement. The turn-58 monitor logged `third_site still incomplete beyond rollout horizon`, while `since_score_change` was only 3: the separate economic milestone caught a failure that score progress alone obscured. The old second-placement request was still queued, so no current failure request was actually sent during this run. This concrete recovery defect motivated the final bounded stale-request replacement above; direct tests verify it emits a fresh request and never accumulates more than one retired future. No third dev run was performed.

Across the two permitted dev runs: **win rate 1/2; mean terminal true VP 9.5; mean composite score 0.595**. These are development results, not training scores. The second loss is partial counterevidence: discrete opening scores produced early cities but did not overcome the inherited midgame expansion bottleneck. That remaining limitation is preserved for the next iteration rather than introducing an untested capital-allocation policy into this opening experiment. The final stale-request recovery change was not evaluated with a real model after the two-run limit; it is covered by focused contract checks and preflight.
