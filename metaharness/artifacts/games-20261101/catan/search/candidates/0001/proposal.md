## Hypothesis and trace evidence

The leader fails primarily at the action boundary, amplified by System-2 latency. Its generic observation formatter cuts the JSON at 6,000 characters, before the private hand and authoritative legal menu. Its parser retains only numeric arguments, destroying structured road edges and action IDs. Seed 101's final observation is still `build_initial_road`, revision 3, with legal edges `[16,21]`, `[19,21]`, `[21,43]` and the explicit error `Illegal, malformed, ambiguous, or stale action; choose a current legal_actions ID.` Its digest records 24 posted actions and 25 Astra calls in 1800 seconds for one true VP. Seed 102 repeatedly posts bare `build_road`, uses 16 calls, and ends with the same one VP. All four training seeds have zero completed normal turns.

Hypothesis: direct execution of current legal IDs, with code handling verified arithmetic and Astra choosing a small number of strategic plans, removes this latency/legality trap. A bounded resource-and-route planner should then convert production into points more effectively than asking a slow model about every forced roll, individual discard, and trade.

## Final harness

- Astra selects each of the two opening settlements from a complete legal-ID menu with resource-specific pip production, ports, existing buildings, and our exact hand. Fresh compact prompts avoid the parent's growing session of repeated invalid moves. Output is matched as complete IDs, including safe handling of arbitrary text.
- Code reconstructs public production and the observed road graph. Multi-source Dijkstra finds settlement routes up to three new roads, respecting distance rules, occupied edges, enemy buildings, and remaining pieces. Each project includes the settlement's full resource cost plus its access roads. Cities and development purchases are competing projects, ranked by expected production and estimated funding time; all estimates remain heuristics.
- A bounded breadth-first search finds up to four maritime trades that fully fund a concrete project while preserving its required inputs. Rates come from observed ports; the first executed trade must match a current legal action, including null padding. The complete planner is recomputed after every applied action, so action IDs and resource assumptions are never reused blindly.
- Routine rolls, robber placement, one-card discards and development plays use live legal choices. Robber valuation includes city multipliers, own production loss and public opponent threat. Monopoly estimates aggregate opponent resources from public bank conservation. Immediate one-point winning builds override economic rankings. Road spending requires a productive settlement destination rather than a free-standing road-building rule.
- `ctx.progress()` is read before and after every action. The harness checks revision advance, rejection count, building/road ownership and exact trade hand deltas. A persistent target becoming unavailable, failed effect check, or 30 actions without score progress triggers Astra replanning with the evidence, public race, current menu and computed project shortlist. Astra can change the persistent project as well as the next action. Replanning is bounded to six calls, uses a 16-action cooldown for ordinary stagnation, and keeps 290 seconds in reserve; calls have 240-second timeouts, above the archive's 182-second maximum. Timeout/backend failures and garbage responses fall back safely to the local planner. Four consecutive execution failures stop the episode instead of spinning.

## Experiments and limitations

The first prototype completed dev seed 301 in 44 seconds at four true VP, zero wins, and zero model calls. Its trace exposed two defects: a discard selector threw away scarce inputs before abundant ore, and dictionary-only trade parsing ignored the actual ordered-list maritime parameters. It accumulated 14 ore and failed to convert production. A second prototype completed dev seed 302 cleanly at three VP, zero wins, with two successful model calls. That version still lacked the final resource/route planner and second-opening-call fix. These are diagnostic observations, not final-policy scores.

Both allowed dev evaluations were consumed on those prototypes. The final version passes pre-flight without warnings, completing the stand-in episode at nine VP with two model calls. Its decision function also selects a currently legal action on all 182 nonterminal observations replayed from the two dev traces. Pre-flight and archived-observation replay establish compatibility, not a measured real-model win rate. No training, validation or held-out evaluation was run by this proposal.

## Confirming or refuting the hypothesis

Training should first show normal-turn completion, negligible rejection counts, bounded model latency, and real resource-to-VP conversion rather than the leader's identical setup-only scores. Win rate is primary; mean terminal true VP is secondary. A clean but losing game with high unused resource stocks refutes the economic planning component; low production despite legal execution implicates opening quality. Repeated project invalidation or resource conservation/effect mismatches must appear in replan logs and would refute the graph/trade assumptions. Unseen maps test whether public topology, current production, bounded projects and explicit failure recovery generalize beyond the two observed dev trajectories. Largest Army and cities are supported, but the harness does not perform a full Longest Road award search; cheap award wins are an important remaining weakness.

Repair: the 60-second pre-flight exposed deadline closure being swallowed by generic RuntimeError/action recovery handlers and no cooperative stop inside local search; BudgetExceeded/EpisodeOver now unwind immediately, search checks a local deadline, model calls require sufficient remaining time, and the loop reserves time for the final synchronous bot advance.
