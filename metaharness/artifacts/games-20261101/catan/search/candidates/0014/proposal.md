## Hypothesis

The inherited economic compiler has an all-or-nothing financing constraint that is not a Catan rule: it can trade to pay an entire road-plus-settlement project, but cannot trade to pay just its next road. Naturally produced WOOD+BRICK can advance a route; an otherwise equivalent hand with maritime surplus cannot. Roads and partial resource conversion can bank progress before a seven, and finishing a reached site needs an explicit milestone rather than continual competition with unrelated purchases.

Leader 0009 seed 102 ends at 6 true VP with only its two setup roads and no ordinary settlement. At turn 26 (trace line 34) it has [0,2,4,0,4] in WOOD/BRICK/SHEEP/WHEAT/ORE order and ends the turn: a single SHEEP→WOOD trade could fund the road to open vertex 32, but the whole road-plus-settlement bundle is not funded. It discards four cards at turn 27 (lines 36–39). Seed 103 builds road [11,32] at turn 27 (line 38), then leaves vertex 32 empty until ORANGE settles there by turn 70 (line 82). It subsequently reaches vertex 23 and buys development cards instead of completing that site; RED claims it before the episode ends at 7 VP. These do not prove expansion would win, but expose a missing executable action family and absent completion accountability.

## Change from parent

Keep 0005's opening, public graph, tactical fallback, legal-ID actions and exact trade rules. Introduce a staged expansion contract: choose an open, reachable short-route target, finance each road independently, then finance its settlement. Maritime trades may advance the current milestone even when the full project is not fundable; they preserve useful target resources and reduce discard exposure. Only one expansion is under construction at a time, and a funded city or immediate winning move may supersede it. New opportunities are evaluated using public opponent network distance, never hidden hands. Automatic contracts start only when a milestone can be funded (or a surplus trade can reduce a hand above seven), normally along at most two remaining roads; Astra can explicitly choose a route up to three roads. The whole route is re-derived after every step. An active contract suppresses unrelated spending that would increase its current milestone deficit, but a sufficiently strong funded city and immediate scoring finishes supersede it. This is a staged financing and completion experiment, not a new stochastic ETA estimator.

Each contract records its target, road count, starting turn, deadline and remaining deficits. A milestone expires after six personal rounds without construction progress; the whole job has a fixed eight-round allowance plus two rounds per road. Failed targets receive a four-round cooldown. One bounded pending inference may be superseded by a new evidence-bearing recovery request, with at most two outstanding model calls; superseded answers are ignored. After every action, rederive its route and check buildings, roads, trades, legal rejection and ctx.progress(). A lost site, lengthened route, uncompleted milestone at expiry, or contradicting action effect releases the contract, puts the failed target on cooldown and sends evidence to Astra. Model answers can select or abandon a contract, alter mode and recover with a current legal action. Delayed action IDs never execute after their revision. Garbage responses and call failures retain legal local play. Calls use a 300-second timeout, above the recent 134-second maximum, with bounded foreground waiting and an episode reserve.

## Confirmation and refutation

Confirm with actual trades funding roads that the parent could not finance, subsequent ordinary settlements at those targets, fewer discarded surplus cards, earlier production growth and improved win rate/weak-seed true VP. Refute if financed roads remain stranded, contracts repeatedly expire, saving suppresses stronger city/Army paths, or production grows without improving race outcomes. Log target acquisition, staged funding, milestone completion, abandonment and model recovery so later iterations can separate compiler reachability from strategic quality. This is not a claim that every expansion beats development cards or cities.

## Validation

Final contract preflight passed with 54 controlled actions and no exception; the harness returned at its safety reserve with 6 VP. No System-2 response was answered, so these are smoke-test results, not competitive performance. Deadline checks reserve at least 45 seconds before entering synchronous opponent advances and include real monotonic time as well as the context budget, incorporating the runtime lesson from candidate 0012 without adopting its economic forecast.

Public archive replay covered 649 nonterminal states from all training traces of 0005 and 0009. Every selected action was in its current legal menu. All 111 returned stage funding plans met their integer card cost and bank constraints. On leader seed 102 at turn 26, the new executor selects ORE→WOOD to fund the next road instead of ending the turn. Paid action resource deltas matched the described costs on all 85 applicable archived actions. Ten focused model cases covered garbage prose, malformed JSON, non-string output, wrong field types, valid strategy, explicit cancellation, TimeoutError and RuntimeError. A deliberately expired construction job cleared its target and generated an Astra prompt containing both the failure and ctx.progress() evidence. These are replay/contract checks, not counterfactual game wins.

Dev evaluation 1, seed 302: **win rate 1/1, terminal true VP 10**, public VP 10, 86 completed turns, 101 controlled actions, 567 seconds, six System-2 calls, no failed calls or episode error. Four logged construction contracts completed: sites 3, 39, 32 and 42. The first two completed at turns 30 and 42 using naturally acquired road resources. At turn 78, event `stage_funding` for `settle:42` explicitly reports `full_ready:false`, full cost [3,3,1,1,0], and stage cost [1,1,0,0,0]; a 2:1 WHEAT→WOOD trade funds road [39,41]. Road [41,42] follows at turn 82 and settlement 42 at turn 86. A fully funded route to settlement 44 then supplies the winning point in the same turn. Three city upgrades and two development purchases remained in the action sequence, so staged construction did not eliminate those paths. This validates the missing financing action family and subsequent completion on one dev board; it does not prove training or unseen win-rate improvement.

After that process loaded the module, final refinements removed unused parent methods, exposed live deficits in the contract, verified paid resource costs explicitly, and let funded winning award/build projects preempt unfinished contracts. The second dev evaluation uses those refinements.

Dev evaluation 2, seed 301: **win rate 1/1, terminal true VP 10**, public VP 9, 65 completed turns, 78 controlled actions, 243 seconds, one queued System-2 call, no failed calls or episode error. The call exceeded the 210-second foreground wait and never returned an answer before the game ended. Thus this episode tests local fallback execution, not Astra's strategic contribution. Three contracts completed at turns 29, 41 and 57. Four road-stage trades explicitly had `full_ready:false`: two toward site 28 and two toward site 30; both sites were subsequently settled. No construction expiration or contradictory postcondition occurred. Recovery is therefore covered by focused tests rather than naturally observed dev failures.

Across the two dev episodes: **win rate 2/2, mean terminal true VP 10.0**, composite mean 1.10, zero errors. They include the minor final refinements noted above and are not a two-seed measurement of one identical file revision. Both show staged road funding followed by settlement completion, but two wins are not evidence of general superiority. The final harness passed preflight again after the immediate-win and resource-cost safeguards. No training or held-out evaluation was run by the proposer.

Limitations: target values and ETAs still inherit the parent's continuous economic heuristics; network distance does not predict an opponent's exact settlement timing. Construction can reserve resources too long or expose roads to a rival. Six-round milestone deadlines are risk limits, not calibrated completion forecasts. The model may choose a poor target or remain queued, and the fallback must then act from public state. Deferred cards/cities and cost of stranded roads remain the main refutation signals for training and unseen seeds.
