Hypothesis: a guess should be valued by progress after subsequent observation-conditioned play, rather than immediate flood size divided by mine risk. A large predicted flood can still leave a sequence of guesses. We test a finite-horizon continuation policy that simulates the actual observable clues, exhausts safe deductions after each reveal, and averages the safe cells gained before loss or three risky reveals.

Evidence: candidate 0006's seed607 reveals (0,0) and (15,15), each showing 1, then loses at (0,15) with only 2/216 safe cells. Its exact risks for the latter two moves are 39/252 and 38/248, so this is not evidence of broken arithmetic. Parent 0005 improves the selection objective but relocates the early failure: trace_seed610 predicts conditional gain 12.286 at (11,10) with risk .186440678, observes just one new safe cell, then predicts gain 12.192 at (11,0) with risk .18695652 and loses at 2/195. These outcomes motivate a longer-horizon experiment; neither single loss establishes a statistically inferior choice.

Changes: preserve the parent's corner opening, exact residual/subset proofs, global completion weighting, and posterior sampling. Replace the one-clue gain/risk objective with bounded posterior policy rollouts. Simulated decisions receive only simulated visible observations; sampled mines are used exclusively by the simulator to generate clue and flood feedback. After each risky reveal, simulate all forced safe moves, then let the existing minimum-risk policy choose the next gamble. Compare expected additional safe reveals over three guesses. Keep root risks exact, never promote sampled certainty into the live proof state, and retain a bounded fallback on incomplete posterior enumeration.

Confirmation: rollout diagnostics should distinguish candidates whose first reveal looks similar but whose later deductions differ; lower early-loss frequency and higher progress should accompany that distinction. Refutation: no improvement beyond Monte Carlo noise, systematic preference for immediate floods despite longer rollouts, or computation limits preventing enough balanced samples. Only the evaluator will run the full training evaluation.

Implementation details: 384 posterior completions screen a geometrically diverse candidate set within six percentage points of the minimum exact mine risk. The finalists include up to five flood opportunities, two cells whose clues overlap the existing frontier, and the baseline. Up to 256 balanced rounds evaluate every finalist on the same sampled completion. Only complete rounds enter the estimate. The objective is exact root survival probability times the mean subsequent safe-cell gain conditional on surviving the root, discounted by half a standard error. A 45-second cap (reduced when the episode deadline approaches), cancellation checks, and a bounded policy-state cache control cost. Fewer than twelve complete rounds use the immediate-progress screen; incomplete root component enumeration uses the inherited minimum-risk fallback.

The simulator flood-fills zero regions and exposes their numbered borders. Its continuation policy recomputes residual/subset deductions, reveals all provable safe cells, and uses the globally weighted minimum-risk policy for the next gamble. Known mines and cache entries are local to the simulation; none enter the live proof state. Exactly inferred safe cells do not consume the three-gamble horizon. The first action and all live safe deductions remain unchanged. No Astra calls, model parsing, flags, background work, seed access, or cross-episode state are introduced.

Validation: the final required preflight passed, winning its stand-in episode in 38 actions with zero model calls. Both permitted dev runs completed without errors. The initial 64-round implementation won both dev seeds; logs showed standard errors around 4–11 cells, motivating increased sampling before the second run. The final 256-round version won 2403 in 34 actions (6.20 seconds) and 2404 in 114 actions (10.87 seconds), with zero model calls. Results and complete traces are in `dev/20261030-185643/`. Each final planning decision completed all 256 rounds, taking 6.107 and 10.671 seconds.

The final 2403 decision differs from the immediate-flood screen: the screen prefers (9,6), while continuation selects baseline (9,11), with expected gains 69.3712 versus 72.1318 at identical risk .137931. On 2404 it selects (0,10), estimating 93.2601 subsequent safe reveals versus baseline 87.1482 at identical risk .182203; the estimates' standard errors are about five cells. These differences are small enough that sampling noise remains material. The two wins establish feasibility, not generalization or a statistically significant policy improvement.

Synthetic-only verification (`verify_synthetic.py`) compared marginal probabilities and proof deductions against brute force on 100 small constraint systems; all passed. An independent queue-based flood implementation and 436 continuations over all two-mine layouts on a small artificial board verified simulator feedback, gain bounds, shared-cache equivalence to uncached decisions, and cancellation. No game implementation or hidden training layouts were used by the harness or synthetic checks.

Limitations: this is evaluation of a fixed minimum-risk continuation policy, not optimal Bellman search. The three-gamble horizon can undervalue later information. Finite posterior samples and candidate screening can miss good alternatives. Root sampling requires every frontier component to enumerate under the inherited 28-cell/time/node bounds; otherwise planning is skipped. The inherited fallback and simulated future probability policy can relax constraints from oversized components, so their estimates are approximate. Exact logical facts remain separate from sampled outcomes. Full training evaluation is left to the evaluator.
