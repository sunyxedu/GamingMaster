Hypothesis: the leader's residual losses are partly an action compression problem. Candidate 0008 proves mines but keeps them as private facts, then reveals each forced safe cell separately. The adaptor's chord operation can open all neighbours of a number in one turn once the exact mine count is flagged. Posting only code-proven mines and chording those clues should shorten deterministic frontiers and reduce the number of observations before an unavoidable guess; it may also avoid late-game tie states by exposing the remaining frontier sooner.

Evidence: candidate 0008 is nearly perfect overall, but trace_seed611 reaches 84/85 safe cells and then loses on a genuine 50/50 choice after two final single-cell reveals. Earlier candidates also spend many turns on safe cells after residual deductions. The trace contains no flags or chords despite the solver having exact mine deductions. This does not show that the final coin flip was avoidable; it motivates testing whether exposing proven frontiers in larger atomic actions changes the state reached before guessing.

What changed vs. the parent: the corner opening, exact residual/subset closure, globally weighted component probabilities, posterior continuation planner, and model-free fallback are retained. After every closure, the harness considers newly proven mines that touch a revealed clue and queues only flags that can enable a profitable chord. It then scans revealed clues for a chord whose adjacent flag count exactly equals the clue, preferring the chord that opens the most hidden neighbours. Chords are attempted before individually revealing proven safe cells. Flags are never inferred from posterior samples or visible annotations; only the current episode's exact closure can create them. If a flag or chord is rejected, compression is disabled and the normal observation-conditioned loop replans.

The intended tradeoff is fewer actions and earlier flood-fill feedback at the cost of annotation turns. Chording cannot improve safety when the proof is wrong, so it is deliberately gated by exact equations and by the adaptor-visible flag set. The live proof state remains separate from sampled worlds, and no model calls or cross-episode state are added.

Evidence that would confirm the hypothesis: traces should show proven mines being flagged, successful multi-cell chords, fewer safe-cell actions per frontier, and either fewer late 50/50 losses or equal progress with materially fewer actions. The score should stay at least as high as 0008 on seeds where the parent already wins. Refutation would be chords rarely becoming available, flag turns consuming more opportunities than they save, or unchanged losses and action counts. A chord that exposes a mine would indicate a bookkeeping bug and would invalidate the approach; the preflight and dev traces should show no such terminal caused by a proven flag.

Validation: the required preflight passed with an arbitrary stand-in model, score 1.0 in 20 actions, and no contract errors. A synthetic scheduler check over 500 random visible states found no unsafe chord, and verified shared-flag cost accounting plus speculative-flag rejection. One permitted dev evaluation completed both seeds without errors: seed 2403 won in 27 actions and seed 2404 won in 83 actions, with zero model calls. Traces show successful flag/chord sequences (for example, a flag followed by a chord opening 29 safe cells on seed 2403) and no rejected compression action. These two wins validate interface compatibility and the intended action compression, not generalization; the evaluator will compare training score first, then System-2 calls and actions.
