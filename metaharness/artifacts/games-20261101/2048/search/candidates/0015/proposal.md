Hypothesis: the 256 plateau is caused by brittle commitment to a single realized RNG trajectory, rather than by insufficient attempts to acquire extra 4 tiles. Candidate 0011 verified its replay and increased final mass, but this can select layouts that look excellent for the predicted spawn and fail when a tile lands in an inconvenient empty cell. A policy that scores all spawn locations and becomes explicitly risk-sensitive under space pressure should preserve the corner stack on unseen seeds.

What changed vs. the parent: this harness does not replay or branch on the public random stream and does not optimize mass. It uses exact local transitions, then evaluates every possible 2/4 placement after each candidate move. With more than six empties it uses a weighted expectation; at six or fewer it mixes that expectation with the worst spawn outcome. A persistent anchor is retained with hysteresis and may move only when the current corner has lost the maximum tile. A bounded two-ply continuation search ranks each root action, and only one verified action is committed before observing the real board. Astra is asynchronous and supplies only anchor/mode guidance after progress, rejection, or scoreless-run evidence; malformed or slow replies cannot stop local play.

Evidence that would confirm the hypothesis is a higher minimum seed score, fewer late-game zero-gain runs, and final boards whose largest tile remains at the selected corner despite varied spawn positions. A 512 tile or a higher merge score without increased mass would be especially strong evidence. If this controller dies earlier, or its scores remain at the 256 plateau while its corner and empty-cell statistics improve, then spawn-location robustness is not the bottleneck and the resource-acquisition hypothesis should be revisited. If all training seeds improve but an unseen seed fails, the hysteresis rule is too rigid and should be replaced with a wider anchor ensemble.
