Hypothesis: game progress must not depend on model availability. An observation-driven controller with an asynchronous strategic adviser can achieve substantially more than a controller that waits for Astra before each action or objective. Model timeouts alone do not protect the episode from admission/queue delays. The distinct structural experiment here is that an unanswered model request never stops exploration, crafting, survival or achievement collection.

Evidence from the archive: baseline trace_seed1106.jsonl and trace_seed1108.jsonl contain one model request followed by a deadline and episode_end with `steps: 0`, `score: 0.0`. Seed 1104 ends with seven movement steps, zero achievements and health 9; survival was not its initial bottleneck. Even seed 1101 executes only 29 steps in 1800 seconds. Its steps 16/17 verify table cost two wood and wood-pickaxe cost one wood; steps 25/26 show that a blocked movement still changes facing.

Evidence from this proposal's dev runs strengthened the latency hypothesis: in dev/20261030-093134, seed 3301 takes 731.53 seconds from its first model request to its next action, although the model_call reports only 22.48 seconds of latency. Seed 3302 takes 380.32 seconds with 20.60 seconds reported model latency. Both requests specified a 150-second timeout. The final harness therefore launches at most one ctx.spawn model request at a time and keeps stepping the turn game. The background function only requests/parses advice; the main thread applies completed results. There are no independent background loops, and the evaluator can cancel the pending model call when the episode ends.

What changed versus the baseline design:

- Keep an episode-local semantic map and use Dijkstra search over remembered walkable terrain, optionally mining through known obstacles. Re-read every action's observation; never execute an unchecked action batch or memorize seed routes.
- Expand achievement dependencies into gather/place/craft operations. Small resource reserves, nearby replacement workbenches, and explicit placement position/facing plans reduce repeated return trips. Successful inventory deltas verify recipe costs; unsuccessful recipe signatures and placement coordinates are bounded. Unverified costs remain hypotheses that Astra may repair; confirmed costs cannot be overwritten by model advice.
- Handle thirst, hunger, hostile mobs and energy in code. Prioritize a stone sword before the second pickaxe. Begin sleep only in strong daylight with no visible hostile, and reject a sleep whose estimated duration would cross dusk. Plant early; normalize the observed `plant_growing` vocabulary and revisit old crops without treating unseen creatures as stationary obstacles.
- Astra receives compact inventory/achievement/resource summaries, recent transitions, failed recipe attempts and recipe hypotheses. It chooses a bounded objective, not movement commands. Resource discovery immediately completes exploration, collection completes after an inventory gain, and stale objectives older than 160 game ticks are discarded. At most eight consultations begin, after the first 100 ticks and then on stagnation. Garbage replies and model exceptions preserve deterministic control.

Development results and limits:

1. Initial pre-flight passed with 16/22 achievements in 368 steps. The first real dev episode (3301, dev/20261030-092319) reached 9/22 in 226 steps with one Astra call and no error. Its trace exposed an exploration objective that continued for 51 ticks after stone became visible; objectives now stop on discovery.
2. The second and final permitted dev evaluation (dev/20261030-093134) reached 10/22 on each of seeds 3301 and 3302, mean 0.454545, one call each, no errors. Both episodes died after unsafe sleep: seed 3301 lost seven health at tick 146, and seed 3302 lost seven at tick 187. Movement on the following tick did not immediately move the player. This motivated the daylight, duration and visible-hostile sleep guards. It also supplied the queue-delay evidence motivating the final asynchronous adviser.
3. These dev scores belong to earlier revisions. The final asynchronous and sleep-guard revision has not had a third dev evaluation; the two-evaluation limit was respected. Final pre-flight passed with 19/22 achievements (0.863636) in 552 steps, zero errors, and no answered S2 calls. This is a smoke-test result, not a training or dev score. Focused replay/contract checks cover the advisor paths omitted by pre-flight. Eight malformed-response/backend-error fixtures passed; verified recipes resisted model overrides; stale advice was ignored; all 226 recorded ticks were processed with a permanently pending adviser. Both recorded pre-sleep failure states now reject sleep. Replay checks establish controller behavior and robustness, not a counterfactual game score.

Confirming evidence in training: a nonzero score floor regardless of admission latency; hundreds of useful game actions per call; verified progression through stone and iron tools; less time spent returning to crafting stations; and survival through the first night without sleeping hits. Refuting evidence: the autonomous controller dies before receiving useful advice, static priorities explore poorly, or mistaken rule hypotheses persist despite failed-action feedback. An important tradeoff is that a fast episode may finish before any Astra answer arrives. Compare achieved breadth, survival and advisor age rather than counting actions alone.
