Hypothesis: the leader's remaining weakness is objective mismatch. It minimizes the largest immediate feedback bucket, then conditional entropy; the evaluator instead rewards the expected turn of a successful solve. A guess with a somewhat larger worst bucket can be better if it solves immediately for one candidate and leaves many other answers one guess from completion. Explicit finite-horizon reward search should improve this tradeoff without changing the verified rules or introducing answer-frequency priors.

Archive evidence: 0003 solved 12/12 training episodes without errors or Astra calls, with mean 3.50 guesses and a worst score of 0.50. Its seed-0 trace reads `slate -> decor -> bairn -> rerun`, with the last two turns receiving `[gray,gray,gray,yellow,green]` and all green. Seed 5 reads `slate -> acorn -> geese -> issue`; `acorn` receives five gray tiles. These are successful but four-guess paths, not parser failures. The implementation also discards all but the first 1,000 alphabetically sorted noncandidate words when the guess pool exceeds 3,500, and restricts the last two turns to candidate answers. Thus it can miss useful probes as well as optimize the wrong objective. A four-guess trace alone does not prove a mistake: comparisons must average over every answer compatible with the clue, rather than privilege its eventual revealed word.

What changed:

- Retain `slate`, the parent's opener, to isolate continuation search. Recompute the exact answer set from the entire accepted history, using green-first duplicate consumption and public lists. Verify supplied file hashes, reject unknown feedback instead of treating it as gray, and never inspect a seed or private game state.
- Replace one-step minimax with a Bellman objective: with `r` guesses left, an immediate solve earns `r` reward units, a solve next turn earns `r-1`, and failure earns zero. Sum these rewards across equally likely surviving public answers. Divide by candidate count and the game's max guesses to obtain expected game score.
- Construct partitions for every unused legal guess. Deduplicate equivalent partitions and prune using an optimistic bound: at most one answer can solve now, and all others need at least another turn. Search all promising partitions for states of at most 12 answers. Larger states use a diversified beam (reward bound, entropy, worst bucket, and candidate guesses), with exact small-state continuations. Large-state results are therefore heuristic, not global-optimality claims. Even with two turns left a nonanswer probe remains eligible if it improves expected terminal reward.
- Cache feedback rows and subproblems within the current decision. Use an 80-second per-decision limit, shortened by the remaining episode budget. Keep a feasible sequential-answer fallback and the best completed root alternative; cancel computation promptly when `ctx.done` or the local deadline becomes true. Execute one action, observe, then re-plan.
- Verify each action, including an Astra recovery action, through turn advancement, exact history append, feedback membership in the predicted partition, candidate reduction, error fields, and `ctx.progress()` rejection/staleness counters. Hand contradictions to Astra with full observation, evidence, progress, and remaining candidates. Parse only complete legal unused words; use a verified candidate fallback after garbage or timeout. Recovery calls have a 220-second timeout (above observed 136-second recent and 176-second archive maxima) and a three-call cap. No model is needed on the normal path.

Mechanism check: reconstructing the 165 answers compatible with the public first clue in 0003's seed-0 trace, without selecting its eventual answer, the new search chooses `diner`. The parent's `decor` has 46 nonwinning feedback buckets and worst bucket 18; `diner` has 62 nonwinning buckets and worst bucket 29. Both are possible answers. This is the intended structural tradeoff. The bounded continuation search reports aggregate reward 528 / 165 / 6 = 0.53333 expected score for `diner`; this is a planning estimate on the whole clue-compatible set, not a measured training score or a proof that the alternative beats `decor`.

Mean score 0.58333; zero model calls, errors, or failed calls; approximately 44 and 27 seconds. The digest and traces were inspected. Two dev games do not establish a training or unseen-seed improvement.

Confirmation would be a higher training mean with no errors, accompanied by cheaper continuation trees across broad clue-compatible answer sets, followed by similar unseen-seed behavior. Refutation would be lower mean reward despite correct state tracking, increased six-turn failures from the wider worst-case branches, or frequent search cutoffs returning the sequential fallback. Then investigate beam omissions and solve-probability tradeoffs before changing the opener or adding model calls. The main limitation is bounded large-state search: finishing the beam does not mean exhaustive optimality.
