Hypothesis

Broad root proof search can spend its deadline on optimistic reward bounds before comparing enough useful first actions. Prioritizing balanced root partitions in a bounded first pass may produce a strong attainable decision earlier; deferred roots can then use the remaining budget to improve or certify that decision. This tests search scheduling with parent 0015, not leader 0012's tail objective or opener selection. Keep the parent's `soare` opener, scalar expected-reward objective, complete legal action space, and 150-second guard fixed.

Archive evidence

The leader is not failing its interface: 0012's eval.json has no errors, all twelve solves, median score 0.667 and minimum 0.500. Its seed-0 trace reports `candidates:165, guess:doric, reward_sum:517, finished:false, beamed:true` at 120 seconds, followed by `bumfs` over seven survivors and `rerun` on guess four. Seed 3 finishes a beam at 61 candidates with `neper`, then uses `colby` over ten survivors and solves `helix` on guess four. These paths show correct elimination with a computation-limited early decision; they do not establish that a particular guess was avoidable. The leader's two-seed dev mean is only 0.50, so its training advantage is not sufficient evidence to copy its objective.

Parent 0015's seed-0 trace is more specific: after `soare`, the 117-candidate search logs `guess:denet, attainable_reward:369, upper_bound:379, gap:10, exact:false, root_actions:10405, root_tested:228` at 150 seconds. It then guesses `reign` and `rerun`, solving in four. The parent's seed-3 root, by contrast, finishes with reward 410 and zero gap after considering only one competitive root action. The hypothesis concerns large unresolved root catalogs, not every four-guess solve. Parent roots are already ordered by reward upper bound and collision count; the alternative tests whether these optimistic bounds over-prioritize hard continuations. No trace proves that hypothesis yet.

Changes versus parent

- Preserve public-list loading/hash checks, duplicate-aware green-first feedback, full accepted-history filtering, the uniform answer prior, `soare`, reward recurrence, and the 150-second decision guard with its wall-time reserve. No seed, target, frequency corpus, or cross-episode cache enters decisions.
- Build the full behavioral partition catalog as before. When the state has more than 24 answers and more than 384 unresolved root records, promote the 384 best by largest residual bucket, squared bucket sizes, possible-answer status, then lexical order. Search that first pass in the parent's reward-bound order, then search ALL deferred records in reward-bound order. Easily solved root actions are still evaluated during the initial scan. The candidate-only attainable incumbent remains available throughout.
- Apply scheduling ONLY to root decision records. Every hypothetical child receives the complete inherited legal-action catalog and uses the unchanged exact quotient solver. Small real states use the parent's ordering. Recompute from actual feedback after each action. This avoids 0011's restriction of future moves to candidate guesses and introduces no permanent beam exclusion.
- Because the two-phase root order is not globally sorted by reward bound, compute a suffix maximum over all pending roots. Check it at each root and use `continue`, not the parent's global early `break`, when an individual bound is dominated. The logged global upper bound therefore includes deferred roots during interruptions. `finished` and `exact` remain distinct; logs add first-pass reward/time and deferred-action work.
- Execute and verify one action at a time, including Astra recovery actions. Check turn advancement, complete history append, predicted feedback membership, candidate reduction, error fields, and ctx.progress() rejection/staleness counters. Contradictions go to Astra with observation, evidence, progress, and candidate examples. Recovery calls catch timeout/runtime failures and arbitrary text, validate legal unused words, and have bounded fallback attempts.

Confirmation / refutation

Support would be better attainable reward at the same computation budget, an earlier strong incumbent, or fewer late solves without a lower mean score. Completing a first pass alone is not an improvement: compare logged reward, global gap, and actual solve counts. Refutation would be slower incumbent discovery or more late solves because useful unbalanced roots are deferred. If a search finishes, its value is the same global optimum as the parent; only tie choices may differ. Under interruption, ordering can improve or worsen the answer. Unseen seeds remain necessary; a two-game dev result is not a training or held-out gain.

The final hypothesis remains unconfirmed on the two dev scenarios, where both search orders finish. The conditional audit demonstrates a strong first pass and correct subsequent proof, but does not isolate host-load effects or prove earlier incumbent discovery than the parent. The intended test for the evaluator is whether the changed root order improves attainable reward when the common 150-second guard interrupts a difficult unseen posterior.
