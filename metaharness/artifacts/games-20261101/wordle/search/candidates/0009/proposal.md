Hypothesis: action-space truncation and depth-first work allocation leave score on the table even with the correct finite-horizon objective. The parent evaluates a fixed beam of at most 30 large-state actions and can hit its time cap while exploring that beam. An all-action branch-and-bound search, seeded with a complete inexpensive policy and reporting an optimality gap, can spend the available time proving or improving the decision instead of silently discarding promising probes.

Concrete archive evidence: candidate 0006's seed-0 trace records `candidates:117, guess:deter, remaining:5, exact:false, stopped:true`; it then needs `cupid -> rerun`, four guesses total. Seed 17 similarly reports `candidates:68, guess:pedal, remaining:5, exact:false, stopped:true`, followed by `cheat -> tweak`, again four guesses. The leader 0004 also logs `candidates:165, guess:diner, total_reward:528, search_finished:false, nodes:106` on seed 0, scoring 0.50. These are accepted, consistent actions; the evidence supports a search-completeness hypothesis, not a parser fix. It does not establish that the selected guesses were suboptimal.

Changes versus 0006:

- Keep the parent's `soare` opener and uniform public-answer prior, isolating continuation search. Read the public word files with hash verification and recompute duplicate-aware candidates from the entire accepted history.
- Remove the large-state beam and greedy depth cutoff. Search every distinct promising legal-action partition at every state. Candidate guesses are scanned before probes, equivalent partitions are merged, and an action is discarded only when its optimistic reward cannot beat the incumbent or it provides no information.
- Construct an executable candidate-only minimax policy first. Its complete simulated tree supplies an attainable incumbent, including failures at the horizon. This policy remains available when the expensive search stops. Singleton/pair branches and the two-turn horizon have exact closed forms.
- Use reward bounds to order and prune actions: an immediate solve contributes r; a state of n answers can earn at most `(r-1)*n+1`, since only one answer can solve immediately. Child bounds sum to an action bound. Explore actions in decreasing bound order and prune partially evaluated actions when even their unvisited branches cannot beat the incumbent. Memoize only fully resolved subproblems.
- A 150-second decision limit, shortened by the episode budget, returns the best fully evaluated root policy. Log its lower bound, remaining upper bound, gap, number of resolved root actions, feedback rows, and whether optimality was proved. Exactness means all omitted actions were bounded out, not merely that a beam finished. Search checks ctx.done and remaining wall time regularly.
- Preserve the parent's observation/progress verifier and bounded Astra recovery. Every action, including a model recovery action, must advance the turn and append the posted word, preserve prior history, and produce a predicted feedback branch. Recovery catches model failures and rejects garbage/repeated/illegal guesses.

Confirmation would be improved training mean without errors, accompanied by previously incomplete clue states reaching gap zero or a smaller certified gap. Refutation would be equal/lower score despite more complete search, or frequent timeouts before attaining a useful incumbent. The full legal action space may prove too expensive on broad states; a large gap makes that limitation visible. Final unseen evaluation remains necessary because a training four-guess branch alone is not evidence of a bad action.

The candidate retains `soare` and changes continuation-search completeness, leaving opener selection to the parallel proposals. No seed-dependent behavior, hidden-state access, shared cross-episode cache, or model arithmetic is used.

- Independent count-based feedback matched all 400 synthetic pairs. An exhaustive oracle matched the chosen action and value on 128 small state/horizon cases, plus nine harder shared-suffix cases. Thirty-six forced-interruption checks retained bounds containing the independent optimum. Twelve fake-context episodes exercised normal execution, rejection, unchanged observation, action exceptions, garbage replies, and model timeouts.

At the 150-second guard it chose `denet`, with attainable reward sum 369 and upper bound 374, after resolving 885 root alternatives and visiting 2,692 states. The difficult state remains unproved; the measured advance is a useful incumbent and explicit bound, not a claim of global optimality.

One dev invocation was used; no full training evaluation was run. The final recovery-only refinement includes progress counters in the Astra prompt and explicitly rejects non-five-letter JSON guesses even if a public legal list is unavailable.
