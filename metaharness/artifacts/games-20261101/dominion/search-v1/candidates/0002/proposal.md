Hypothesis: a small money/attack specialist with selective tactical criticism can complete games and outperform open-ended construction against the disclosed single-buy money opponents. Astra should spend inference on current purchase forks while code executes a coherent restricted strategy. This tests restricted strategy breadth and event-triggered criticism, rather than reusable target-deck plans (slot 1) or sampled full-game rollouts (slot 3).

Archive evidence: every baseline episode reaches the 1800-second deadline without completing. In trace_seed1301.jsonl, the final observation is turn 13, six Provinces remain, and margin is -14. The agent owns Chapel, Festival, Laboratory, Militia, two Sentries and Witch but only one Gold; the opponent already owns five Golds and two Provinces. This does not establish that engines are bad—the game is unfinished and thinning is corrupted—but motivates testing an economy with fewer moving parts. The same trace line 15 returns `choose 5 0 1 2 3` while line 16 posts only `choose 5 0`; a 224.9-second Sentry pass at line 87 demonstrates avoidable per-prompt inference overhead.

Final implementation: a fresh standard-library controller defaults to money/attack on every kingdom. It buys early thinning and one effective attack/draw terminal, then money and limited cantrip support. Astra critiques at most seven milestone purchases, receives a fresh public-state snapshot and locally computed candidate features, and returns a validated current selection. It may explicitly select a Gardens/Workshop specialist when those cards exist; Gardens is not the default. The rush specialist opens Workshops, acquires Gardens, and targets Gardens, Estates and Workshops as its three piles. No future option IDs or executable code are accepted. Invalid advice cannot change the persistent specialist.

Routine prompts use local selection, including subset search for multi-card Chapel trashing and retained-hand search for forced discards. Current-turn affordable basket search can secure winning Province/three-pile endings, including Gardens thresholds and turns-started tiebreaks; it is bounded to six buys and 12000 nodes. Failed/malformed critiques preserve deterministic control; two failed calls disable further reviews. Each model request has a 150-second timeout. Requests use `ctx.spawn` solely so the main control loop can independently limit total waiting, including admission/queue delay. At 180 seconds of waiting, or 120 seconds remaining in the episode, it abandons further critiques and completes locally. The evaluator handles cancellation of the pending model call at episode end; there is no background simulation.

Development evidence: the initial synchronous dev run remained at its first model request beyond 150 seconds without any model completion or action. It was stopped as superseded after roughly seven minutes; no score is claimed for that run. The second and final allowed dev run, under dev/20261030-002420, exercised the independent wait bound and the then-default Gardens rush. Both episodes completed in 180.6 seconds, with zero errors, zero illegal choices and no truncation. Scores were -21 and -12 (mean -16.5), with agent VP 51 in each. Each requested one model call, but neither received advice before the wait cutoff, so this tests fallback play and completion, not Astra's critique quality.

The dev digest and traces refuted the automatic rush default: in seed3100 Gardens emptied by turn 12, but early scoring diluted the deck and Workshop gains arrived too slowly. By turn 25 the agent still had nine Workshops; it was behind and could not safely finish the third pile. The opponent eventually scored 72 VP. The final revision therefore uses money/attack as the fallback on every board and permits Gardens only by a valid critic choice. That final default change has not received another real-model dev evaluation because both allowed evaluations were used. No training or held-out evaluation was run.

Validation: final preflight PASS, completing its short test at +8 VP in 30 actions. Its short budget skips Astra, so this is not a model-policy performance claim. check_policy.py checks all 140 archived public prompts, the four-card Chapel regression, Gardens scoring thresholds, affordable Province+Duchy finishing baskets, turns-started tiebreaks, ten valid/garbage/failed critic-response classes, the pending-call reserve cutoff, and a malformed critique that must not change the specialist. A synthetic public-state regression checks the Gardens third-pile target. All checks pass on the final module.

Confirmation: complete training games with no illegal actions/errors, at most seven reviews, coherent economies, and improved actual terminal margins. Refutation: completed games still lose because the restricted specialist misses engine opportunities, tactical critiques derail its economy, or the optional rush repeats the observed dilution failure. The finite basket search and hand-value heuristics are approximations, not optimal play or knowledge of hidden deck order. The dev results support the independent wait bound, reject the original automatic Gardens choice, and leave the value of real Astra critiques unmeasured.
