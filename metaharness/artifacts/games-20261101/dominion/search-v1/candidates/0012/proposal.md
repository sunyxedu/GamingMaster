Hypothesis: the portfolio can keep adding draw and action cards after the realized deck has outrun its money payload. A closed-loop payload controller should repair that imbalance with Treasure before allowing more engine construction. This tests a different failure mode from the parallel Province-harvesting proposal: it reacts to the agent's owned composition, without using opponent Province ownership or a new race threshold.

Evidence: candidate 0008's worst episode, `trace_seed1201.jsonl`, shows the agent already behind (public VP 0–21) with five Provinces left at turn 11, then buying Laboratory at $8. It buys Market at $9 on turn 13 and three more Markets by turn 14 while owning no Gold. The owned ledger at that point is two Laboratories, four Markets, one Militia, three Silvers and two Villages (plus Chapel); its computed Treasure payload is only $12 for a roughly 13-card acquired deck. It finally starts buying green cards at turn 15, after the opponent reaches five Provinces, and loses 17–50. The trace demonstrates overbuilding and a payload deficit; it does not establish that every Laboratory purchase was wrong or that a fixed Province trigger is optimal.

What changed versus 0009: I retained its legal prompt executor, complete-policy comparison, independent public-belief rollouts, exact ending handling, constrained Mine/Remodel choices, bounded asynchronous Astra advice, and garbage/error recovery. Before every decision, the new `refresh_mode` computes owned-card count, Treasure payload (Copper/Silver/Gold plus Militia/Festival/Market money), and draw additions from the public ledger. Once a deck has at least twelve cards and payload is below both a fixed minimum and its card-count demand, it enters `payload` repair mode. In that mode the live buy is chosen by a separate local controller: affordable Gold or Silver is preferred, a single terminal payload card is allowed if none exists, and further Laboratory/Market/Village construction is suppressed. Province is still taken when it is already a near-end pile; normal portfolio search resumes only after payload catches up. The mode is recomputed after every step, so a successful Gold/Silver purchase can release it without stale plan state.

The mode is deliberately based on public ownership rather than hidden deck order, opponent identity, seed, or kingdom ID. It is a structural feedback loop: the same portfolio can build when its realized payload supports the deck and repair when it does not. Existing search remains available whenever the balance is healthy, and all selected labels are rebuilt from the current prompt occurrence IDs.

Confirmation would be completed games with no errors or illegal actions, fewer consecutive turns buying draw/action cards while Gold is absent, and improved VP margins on Chapel/engine episodes without reducing margins on money boards. Traces should show a repair entry followed by Gold/Silver acquisition and a return to normal policy after the payload catches up. Refutation would be systematic early mode entry that delays Provinces or useful thinning, no payload recovery because the mode cannot afford Treasure, or lower margins despite fewer overbuilt turns. If refuted, the likely issue is that aggregate owned payload is too coarse a proxy for hand-level draw quality; a future test should condition repair on observed turn money and shuffle timing.
