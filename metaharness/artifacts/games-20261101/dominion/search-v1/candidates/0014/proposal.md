Hypothesis: aggregate owned-card payload is too slow and too coarse to control an engine. In the worst archived game, the agent’s public state was already 21 VP behind with five Provinces left, yet it continued Laboratory/Market construction; ownership totals could not distinguish a productive draw from a bad hand. A turn’s realized coin total is direct evidence of whether the current shuffle converted actions into payload. A controller that commits the whole turn to scoring after a strong draw, or to treasure repair after a weak draw, should stop buy-by-buy oscillation and prevent another cantrip purchase after a failed engine turn.

What changed versus 0012: I retained its legal executor, exact ending checks, constrained Mine/Remodel choices, portfolio simulation, asynchronous Astra recovery, and payload hysteresis. I added a turn-level commitment derived from the live buy prompt and the previous turn’s realized income. When the opponent has a public Province lead and the current turn can afford green, the turn is locked to Province/Duchy/Estate scoring. After a weak prior turn (under $5) the next turn is locked to Gold/Silver repair; the existing payload lock remains a fallback. The commitment is held across nested prompts and multiple buys, so a later action prompt or a second buy cannot re-enable engine construction. Every selected card is still rebuilt from current option IDs and validated locally; model output remains advisory and cannot change legality.

Evidence that would confirm this hypothesis is a lower frequency of consecutive Laboratory/Market buys after low-income turns, earlier Province purchases when the opponent owns three or more Provinces, and improved margins on the archived Chapel/SM failure without harming money boards. Refutation would be frequent score locks when a green card is not actually affordable, repair turns that delay a winning Province, or unchanged losses despite the new commitments; that would mean realized income is too noisy and shuffle-aware state is needed instead.
