Hypothesis: the baseline's main failure is inference bandwidth, but a batch of model-authored commands is still too fragile because every Dominion turn can branch into a nested discard, trash, gain, or attack prompt. The better structural split is to ask Astra only for a board-level regime and let a receding-horizon local controller own routine legal choices. Public race signals and progress failures should be enough to switch regimes or request another plan before a stale local policy costs the game. The archive gives a concrete bandwidth failure: `trace_seed1201.jsonl` ends at -16 after 24 actions and 25 System-2 calls at the deadline, while `trace_seed1100.jsonl` needs 26 calls and 1654 seconds to finish even though it wins by 6.

What changed vs. the parent: the harness makes one compact strategic call at the opening, with later calls only after a six-turn review, a Province-race transition, or repeated rejected/unchanged actions. It parses a small JSON regime (money, engine, Witch, or Gardens plus targets), then ranks current legal options from exact prompt data and visible counts. It rebuilds occurrence IDs on every prompt, handles nested card prompts with current min/max bounds, prefers action-capacity/draw before terminals, and buys Provinces/late Duchies immediately. It invalidates bad local choices using `ctx.progress()`, remembers rejected `(decision_id, action)` pairs, and falls back to a fresh legal response. Model failures and arbitrary preflight text are contained.

Evidence that would confirm or refute it: compared with 0000, episodes should use single-digit Astra calls, complete substantially more turns before the deadline, and improve the weak 1201/1401-style margins. Traces should show the regime changing when Provinces become scarce and a replanning call after a rejected or unchanged action. Refutation would be a large margin loss on Chapel/engine or Gardens boards despite finishing games, or repeated local stalls; that would indicate the regime needs more frequent Astra control or that nested choices need specialized local rules.
