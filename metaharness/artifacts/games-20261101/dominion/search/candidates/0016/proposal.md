Hypothesis: the leader's public-zone rollout model is useful, but its exchangeable opponent-zone assignment discards public evidence. It can put just-played Treasures and just-bought Smithies into the opponent's already-drawn hand, changing estimated attack value and race pace. Conditioning on provable public discard membership and cleanup types should improve forecasts while preserving the leader's search and strategy.

Concrete evidence: in the leader's worst training episode, `trace_seed1400.jsonl` (final margin -13), the transition to decision 2 records four opponent Copper plays and a Smithy buy. The opponent then has hand_count=5, deck_count=0, discard_count=6. Its first cleanup drew the five remaining starting cards without reshuffling, so Smithy and those four Coppers are certainly in discard. Parent `Rollout.__init__` instead shuffles all eleven owned cards and assigns the first five to hand, giving the newly purchased Smithy a 5/11 chance of appearing immediately. One unplayed cleanup card must be Estate because all Treasures were automatically played. Thus the current hand multiset is publicly implied as Copper×3, Estate×2, although its order remains unknown. Later, decision 17 follows a Province buy without a shuffle; that Province and the played Treasures are also known discard cards. The game buys Duchy at decision 21 and never buys another Province. This does not prove the approximation caused the loss; it identifies a falsifiable forecast error within that loss.

Track anonymous unplayed cleanup slots too: they exclude Treasures, and exclude Smithy unless one Smithy already consumed the turn's Action. Sample these nested classes without replacement before allocating the remaining hidden zones. If a shuffle may have occurred, forget all old constraints. This is a public-information belief model, not hidden-order reconstruction.
