Hypothesis: candidate 0011’s public-zone Monte Carlo search is useful mainly once the game is visibly a scoring race. Early in the game it has the least reliable evidence: the opponent’s hidden hand/deck is exchangeably assigned, and a purchase cannot affect the next hand until a shuffle. That approximation can make a speculative attack look better than an economy opening (the leader’s trace at seed 1401 selected Militia at $4 while its own baseline was lower), and it spends substantial time repeatedly simulating action purchases. A second protocol defect is that 0011 starts an Astra future but immediately waits up to 320 seconds in `review`, so queue latency still blocks the turn loop.

What changed vs. the parent: I kept the leader’s audited local executor, public-zone conservation checks, exact liquidation, forecast audits, stale-ID validation, and fallback recovery. Rollout search is now phase-gated: through agent turn 3 while more than five Provinces remain, it uses the local opening policy; in the midgame, the estimator compares the baseline against scoring cards (Province, Duchy, Estate, Gardens and threshold Copper) and leaves action/attack acquisitions to the local policy; it resumes full scoring search under Province pressure. Astra reviews are genuinely asynchronous: a request is spawned with a 260-second model timeout and never synchronously awaited, then parsed only when complete. A failed or stale review cannot block or inject old option IDs.

Evidence that would confirm the hypothesis: lower wall time and fewer early rollout purchases, openings that retain the parent’s stable economy/attack behavior, and improved margins on seeds where early Militia or other speculative actions delay the first reshuffle. Late traces should show rollout choices concentrated on Provinces and exact green liquidation, with no illegal actions or stale IDs. Refutation would be lower margins because the local opening misses a kingdom-specific Witch/Chapel line, or scoring search still predicts poorly after the first shuffle; that would indicate the approximation is structurally wrong rather than merely mistimed.
