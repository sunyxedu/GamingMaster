Hypothesis: a VP deficit is not evidence of imminent game end when it was caused by our own thinning. Moreover, money printed on terminal Actions is not interchangeable with Treasure money when Chapel spends the only Action. Combining those two accounting mistakes can create a feedback loop: premature green purchases, repeated terminal attacks, then removal of the remaining usable economy. This candidate tests cash-flow invariants and causal race detection, using a different parent from the leader.

Concrete archive evidence: the leader 0011's worst game, `trace_seed1400.jsonl`, finished at -13. It bought Duchy at decision 21 ($5, four Provinces), then produced $7/$6/$3/$7/$6/$2/$1 on successive buy turns and never bought another Province. That is a cash-flow failure pattern, but does not alone establish why it happened. Parent 0012 provides a sharper mechanism in `trace_seed1301.jsonl`: decision 5 trashes all three Estates, moving VP from 3 to 0 without an opponent scoring gain; decision 8 then buys Duchy for $6 with **eight Provinces remaining**. Decisions 15, 17, 19 and 23 buy more Militia despite already owning one. At decision 25 Chapel trashes three remaining Coppers from a deck containing five Militia and one Silver. The old reserve counts those mutually competing Militia as $10 of economy. The model later explicitly diagnoses “Repeated stalls and 8 Militia invalidate the opening,” but its Silver/Gold repair cannot override the race branch. The game finishes -25. The same branch lets support purchases bypass their total-ownership caps.

Changes versus 0012:

- Define the thinning reserve from owned Copper/Silver/Gold only. Chapel and Sentry retain at least $4 of this stable Treasure money; Astra may conservatively raise the floor to $5 or $6. A current deck below the floor prioritizes Silver/Gold acquisition over more support. Expected money from mutually exclusive terminal Actions cannot authorize more Copper trashing.
- Apply support ownership caps before every purchase regime. A race does not license repeated Militia or Chapel buys. Require actual opponent Province ownership advantage **and** at most four Provinces before activating the race override. Our own Estate removal no longer starts a Duchy rush at eight Provinces.
- Retain exact late purchase bundles and occurrence-safe prompt execution. Once late scoring starts, do not automatically trash the Estates the scoring policy just bought. Update regimes and rebuild legal IDs after every observation.
- Keep a bounded strategic planner with opening, first Province-race, and evidence-triggered reviews (at most four calls). Requests include stable Treasure money, the reserve, support targets, recent acquisitions, authoritative card text, and current—not stale—bundle candidates. Model calls request 300 seconds and the outer future wait is at most 320 seconds, above the archive's recent 151-second maximum and historical 216 seconds. At least 45 seconds is reserved for local completion. An unresolved future remains tracked; one fresh failure review may supersede it. Stale option IDs are discarded; malformed output and call exceptions preserve the local policy.

The executor verifies `ctx.progress()`, advancing decisions, rejection counts, exact public VP, and observed gain ownership after each relevant step. Low economy or reserve shortfalls carry their concrete evidence to Astra for a repair; the repaired plan is checked by the same loop. Model plans are advisory, not hidden-state estimates. No seed, episode label, or hidden order selects a policy.

Confirmation would be fewer premature Duchies and uncapped terminal acquisitions, preservation of functional Treasure after Chapel, completed legal games, and improved minimum/mean margins across kingdoms. Refutation would be overconservative thinning, delayed scoring, or weaker engine payoffs despite the accounting corrections. The reserve is a conservative heuristic, not a proof that Treasures dominate action payload; a well-built Festival engine could benefit from a lower reserve than this experiment allows.
