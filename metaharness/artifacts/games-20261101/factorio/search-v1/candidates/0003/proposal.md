Hypothesis: a declarative production-goal controller can turn a small number of Astra decisions into a complete factory, because dependency accounting, repeated material handling, and execution feedback belong in code. This tests a structural alternative to a model choosing individual action strings: an observation-driven worker executes persistent construction and inventory goals while Astra chooses power layouts, production milestones, and repairs.

Scope distinction within this parallel iteration: this candidate asks Astra to synthesize the power layout and choose persistent construction/stock goals at runtime. It does not encode a predetermined complete power/red-science build sequence. The initial smelter geometry and routine economy are deterministic; the higher-level factory remains model-designed. The experimental question is whether changing Astra's control abstraction from individual actions to executable goals makes those decisions practical under latency.
