## Hypothesis

The green-science controller is production-capable but becomes copper-starved late in the horizon. Its final dev trace ended with copper at zero while iron plates and red packs remained; the green assembler therefore could not sustain circuits, cables, inserters and belts. The leader's second coal/iron expansion increased upstream capacity, but copper remained a single burner-drill/furnace pair. This tests whether the score ceiling is a raw copper throughput imbalance rather than insufficient laboratories or research choice.

## What changed

I retain the parent's verified direct-feed bootstrap, pump-relative steam layout, recipe compiler, asynchronous exception advisor and green-science assembler. After the first science block and two iron expansion pairs, the plan now compiles one additional copper burner-drill plus stone-furnace pair from the currently observed copper patch rows. The pair is seed-relative and uses the same direct east-facing drill-to-furnace geometry as the established chains. It is deliberately placed after the first red/lab milestone so the extra construction cannot postpone bootstrap electricity and automation.

The normal steward already extracts furnace output before refuelling and feeding machine inputs, so the additional pair shares the existing 100-item transfer ledger without special-case IDs. All recipes, coordinates, terrain/tree clearing, stock reservations and machine inventories continue to come from live observations. Astra remains exception-only; malformed or stale advice is discarded.

## Confirmation / refutation

Confirmation is earlier sustained copper-plate production, fewer turns with empty copper stock, continued green-pack production and a higher logistic-science component than parent 0005 without action errors. Refutation is that the extra pair delays automation enough to lose more early science than it adds, exhausts coal or manual-worker time, collides on a held-out patch, or leaves copper idle because transfer/input capacity is the actual bottleneck. The evaluation should compare copper production and green consumption trajectories, not just the final scalar.
