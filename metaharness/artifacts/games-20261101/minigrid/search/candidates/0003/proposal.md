Hypothesis: the leader fails mainly because its control loop makes one slow Astra call a prerequisite for every action. The archive has two concrete deadline failures: seed 1 and seed 6 each show one `system2 call` followed by a 1,800 s deadline with zero actions. Even successful play pays the same latency repeatedly; seed 2 used 23 Astra calls for 23 actions, and seed 8 includes a 101.6 s call. The model may have enough competence, but the one-action interface leaves too little useful work per call and can strand an episode behind a stalled request.

This candidate keeps the parent’s observation-only interface but changes the control unit from one action to a short, model-produced prefix. Astra receives the complete public observation, recent verified transitions, progress counters, and the previous prefix, and returns JSON containing 4–10 actions. The executor applies one action at a time and records the before/after observations. It clears the prefix and sends the contradiction (rejected action, unchanged pickup/drop/toggle state, or five unchanged public observations) back to Astra for replanning. Malformed output, timeout, exhausted budget, and unavailable inference fall back to a conservative local action policy; model calls have a 155 s bound, above the archive’s 102 s worst observed call. A locked door is toggled locally only when a matching carried key is visible, and open doors are traversed.

This is a structural batching experiment rather than a map/search compiler: the model retains responsibility for mission interpretation and route selection, while code owns prefix parsing, action accounting, and recovery evidence. It should preserve the world-waits-while-thinking advantage without assuming a particular training layout.

Evidence that would confirm the hypothesis is a large reduction in Astra calls and deadline endings, with equal or higher success and fewer wasted turns on both the weak and median seeds. It would be refuted if batches frequently cross a turn, door, or interaction precondition, causing wrong-object or hazard failures and a lower success rate despite fewer calls; repeated batch contradictions or local fallback wandering would also indicate that the prefix horizon is too long or that latency was not the dominant failure.
