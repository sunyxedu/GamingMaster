Hypothesis: the leader's low mean (0.658) is driven primarily by call granularity and an unhandled slow-call failure mode, rather than by Astra being unable to navigate. The traces show seed 1 and seed 6 spending the entire 1,800-second deadline on one call with zero actions, while successful seeds use one Astra call for every action (for example, seed 14 solves in 5 calls and seed 8 in 8). This leaves too little wall time for longer DoorKey and exploration episodes.

What changed vs. the parent: Astra is now a short-horizon planner that returns up to eight actions in one compact line. The harness executes the batch immediately, checks `ctx.progress()` after every action, compares semantic state for manipulation effects, and sends a fresh observation plus the rejection evidence when a plan stalls. Parsing is tolerant of arbitrary stand-in text, and model timeouts/errors fall back to one bounded action instead of escaping the contract. Prompts explicitly preserve unknown cells, mission ordering, and interaction preconditions, while retaining a session so Astra can refine a plan without replaying a full transcript.

Evidence that would confirm or refute it: on disjoint seeds, confirmation is a higher success rate on DoorKey/MultiRoom/PickupLoc and materially fewer System-2 calls per successful episode, with no increase in illegal actions. If batched plans frequently become stale and the rejection-triggered replans do not recover, or if success remains near the leader despite substantially fewer calls, then latency granularity was not the dominant failure and the next iteration should focus on map construction or verifier-specific planning.
