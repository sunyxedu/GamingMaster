## Hypothesis
The clue-only communication channel wastes resources: ordinary discards recover a token but convey no actionable instruction, while direct play clues spend a token. A publicly decoded discard slot can do both jobs in one turn. The extra choice constraint on which hidden card gets discarded should be outweighed by more executable safe plays and lower clue pressure. This is a new action-signaling mechanism, not a refinement of leader 0003's rotating clue recipe.

## Archive evidence
Leader 0003's weakest training trace (seed 14) finishes 20 with three lives. At turn 31 it discards W5/card 1 with one clue, describing `own discard E_loss=0.542, p_dead=0.286`. At turn 37, at zero clues, it discards G3/card 25 after G3/card 13 was discarded on turn 26. The final green height is 2. Its early trace repeatedly uses a color clue followed by a discard (turn pairs 2/3, 4/5, 9/10, 11/12, 13/14, 15/16 and 17/18): two actions merely break even on tokens. Those observations suggest communication economics, not life preservation alone, constrain the current recipes.

Parent 0006 instead retains useful literal knowledge and critical-save promises, but trains at 20.25 versus leader 21.5; its weakest seed 12 finishes 18 with seven clues. Thus more token recovery alone is insufficient. This experiment predicts actionable information carried by the recovery action matters. Parent inference, saving below the token cap, and transport code are retained. The final design also supplies a full-token signaling mode because the first dev trace showed the discard channel was often unavailable.

## Change

- The public fallback discard (parent's public chop) is a reserved neutral symbol. Other own discard indices map to recipient slots whose public domains still permit a playable card but do not already guarantee one. The map uses public marginals, public masks, stable hand order, public promises and a rotating tie break; neither hand's actual faces enter the decoder.
- A mapped discard is offered ONLY if the sender can see the mapped partner card is actually playable. On the accepted public discard event, both seats reconstruct a durable promise: that card's public domain intersected with identities playable at signal time. Stable card IDs preserve the deduction across hand shifts. No sender-visible face is copied into public memory.
- Unmapped discards are neutral; a private proven-dead override is permitted only when its index is not assigned a signal. This restriction is essential: otherwise a harmless own discard could tell the partner to misplay.
- At exactly eight clues, rank clues encode a safe play among publicly eligible recipient slots, using `(rank - 1 + turn // 2) % eligible_count`. Color clues at eight clues are literal-only escape actions. The mode is decoded from the public pre-action token count; below eight, the parent's color-play/rank-save conventions apply.
- Score the combined token recovery, draw and newly executable partner play against the sender's expected discard loss. Exact finite-copy beliefs, endgame expected-score risks and at most two seat-isolated Astra option choices remain from the parent. Every posted action comes from legal_actions; arbitrary replies/timeouts fall back to code.

## Predictions and refutation
Confirm if the new `discard play signal` events are followed by safe scoring plays, reduce play clues per point, and avoid worse lost ceilings. Refute if symbol constraints force costly discards, the public map rarely reaches an actually playable target, or abundant tokens still accompany stranded prerequisite chains. Log mapping targets, expected discard losses, decoded domains and live-last-copy losses. No training seed or hidden engine state is a policy input.

## Validation
The initial harness and proposal were written before evaluation. Both permitted dev runs are documented below; no training or held-out evaluation was run. The final runtime policy is the version tested in the second run.

### First dev observation and channel extension
The initial implementation passed preflight (natural completion, 64 actions, zero errors) and an archive audit of 139 observations: erasing both hands' faces left the discard codebook and public decoder unchanged; all 70 offered signals certified a truly playable visible card, and 519 clue decodes were face-invariant.

The first permitted dev run (`dev/20261030-004158`) scored 19/25 in 69 actions, with all lives and no episode error. Only ONE discard signal was actually sent (turn 5); its recipient safely played B1 on turn 6. This refutes the assumption that the channel is broadly available under the parent's token-rich policy. Most later discards were neutral, repeatedly returning tokens to eight, where discarding is forbidden. At turn 51, a full-token rank-1 clue left Y1/card 37 ambiguous; it was discarded at turn 52. The yellow stack only started on the final action while Y2/Y4/Y5 remained in the partner's hand. W5 was the only live last-copy loss, at turn 14. This is an access failure, not a misplay or exhausted model budget.

The refinement keeps the same action-signaling hypothesis but adds a complementary channel when discards are illegal: at exactly eight tokens, rank clues encode a direct play among publicly possible playable slots (rank ordinal plus public turn rotation). The sender validates the encoded card. Color clues at eight tokens are literal-only escape symbols, ensuring a truthful non-signaling action is always available. Below eight tokens, the parent's color-play/rank-save protocol and the new discard channel apply. The receiver derives the mode from the public pre-action token count. This is a bounded overflow mode, rather than replacing the parent's whole protocol with leader 0003's clue recipe. It specifically tests whether token saturation and oldest-color-focus obstruction caused the observed channel starvation.

### Final validation and limits
The second permitted dev run (`dev/20261030-004718`) finished **21/25**, 63 actions, 80.60 seconds, three lives, eight clues, and no episode error. It used six discard play signals and ten full-token play clues. All six discard signals were followed immediately by successful recipient plays. Yellow completed at turn 60. The observed communication mechanism works, and the second version improved the paired dev deal from 19 to 21; this does **not** establish superiority over parent 0006, whose archived dev score was 23, or over the training leader.

The remaining physical ceiling was exactly 21: last B4/card 22 was discarded neutrally at turn 22 and last G4/card 31 was discarded as a play signal at turn 43. The latter directly exposes the tradeoff: signaling successfully enabled G2 next turn but selected a hidden G4 whose actual two-point loss exceeded its estimated loss 0.3163. Saving only the neutral public chop does not cover every possible signaling discard. A future iteration should test protection/coordination for the entire set of signal-bearing discard slots, or information conveyed by safe existing plays, rather than merely increasing signal scores. This is a genuine unresolved limitation of this experiment.

Final preflight passed with natural completion, 63 legal actions and no errors. `audit.py` passed on 139 archived observations: 70 offered discard signals and 509 clue decodes (including 13 full-token signals) were truthful and invariant to erasing both hands' faces. A separate replay of the final dev transcript reconstructed all 17 persistent promises from face-erased public pre-action states; every recorded tracker matched, and every inferred domain contained the subsequently observed true face. Fake contexts covered garbage/missing/out-of-range choices, model timeout, backend failure, outer queue timeout, and independent seat sessions.

Both dev adviser attempts hit the caught 80-second outer wait; autonomous play completed afterward. No successful model contribution is claimed. The final harness reserves 75 seconds for the backend and bounds caller waiting at 80 seconds; any advisory exception disables further requests. No further dev evaluations were run, and the policy was not changed after the second dev result.
