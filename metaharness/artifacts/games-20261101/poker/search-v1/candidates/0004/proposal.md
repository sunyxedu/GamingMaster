## Hypothesis

The leader's one-street showdown EV undervalues waiting for future public information and future value bets. Against this opponent, a turn call or check buys a river on which the policy can select a different size after seeing the board and the opponent's public action. This option has value even when immediate equity favors a shove. Explicit turn-to-river planning should improve extraction without abandoning the leader's successful large-value-bet exploit.

The weakest archived training match is seed 2207 (+83 chips/hand). Its hand-22 holds Js8c on Td4h9dJc: the turn decision bets 250 with equity .7568, but its recorded `passive_ev` is only 335.4, treating the entire future as discounted showdown. On the Ts river the same code finds a 19500-chip bet with `bet_ev` 3869.8 and `called_equity` .7799. That large future opportunity was absent from the turn comparison. The hand loses to publicly revealed JhQc, which does not itself refute the estimated range EV. Hand-74 similarly changes from a 100-chip turn bet on 8d4h2sTd with Qh2c to a 19550 river bet after Qc (`bet_ev` 7173.2); the river opportunity was not represented in its turn policy. Hand-82's Kc3s flop shove, called by Kh2c and lost on the river, illustrates the remaining earlier-street horizon limitation rather than proof that shoving top pair is wrong.

## What changes

Retain the parent's public-combination posterior, exact hand evaluator, preflop policy, legal action filtering, cancellation checkpoints, and zero model calls. Add an explicit terminal river solver using the documented opponent thresholds, legal integer sizing, and a common hero response for each public betting branch. Retain every exact river slice generated by the turn equity computation and integrate a range-wide optimal river continuation into turn action EV. Future actions may depend on the exposed river and public opponent response, never on a sampled hidden holding. Counterfactual call ranges must be conditioned before solving each future decision.

This is one horizon-extension experiment. Flop policy and preflop sampling stay as controls. All calculations use public cards and hypotheses over unknown cards, never game internals or deal seeds. No Astra prompts are needed because the tested decision rule is a finite numerical search with known opponent rules.

## Confirm / refute

Confirm with completed, legal independent dev matches; traces showing turn choices altered by quantified future value; and positive match-level improvement over the parent. Refute if computation prevents completion, if the river solver fails analytic/replay checks, or if future-value choices systematically miss immediate value. A two-seed development mean is exploratory and not a reliable significance test. Losses in individual all-in hands are not independent evidence of policy quality.

The first pre-flight passed: `ok: true`, 50 actions, no exception, zero model calls. The short pre-flight stopped cleanly at its CPU budget checkpoint.

An offline validation script independently realizes chip payoffs for the selected river policy on 500 random public decision nodes. All match the prefix-moment calculations. Additional checks cover always-winning, always-losing and tied holdings, scaling of unnormalized posterior mass, and clipped bet sizes: when different opponent branches all bet the same short all-in, they are merged before hero chooses a response. The merged example returns 56.2 chips, versus the invalid 76.2 from choosing separately for indistinguishable hidden branches.

## Deliberate approximations

This is a finite action-menu lookahead, not a solved poker game. River response thresholds and terminal card ranks are exact; incoming range weights still use the parent's smoothed likelihoods. Turn hypothetical responses retain that smoothed equity model, and intermediate turn responses search fold/call/shove. The root turn and river decisions use the parent's broad sizing menu and exposure safeguards. River nodes include checking, price-sensitive calls, value/bluff raises, the opponent's one allowed raise, and a common response to that raise. Each holding leaves exactly 44 possible rivers after removal of both players' holes and the four-card board; branch weights are integrated with that denominator. Opponent random-hand equity continues to use its own information set and does not remove hero's private cards.

Flop lookahead and modeling correlation in the opponent's cached Monte Carlo estimate remain future work. No action depends on a training/development seed, a memorized deal, hidden cards, or unrevealed future community cards.
